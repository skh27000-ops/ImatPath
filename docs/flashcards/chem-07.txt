chem07-002	Propane (−42 °C), dimethyl ether CH<sub>3</sub>OCH<sub>3</sub> (−24 °C) and ethanol (78 °C) have similar molar masses (44, 46 and 46). Which explains the order of boiling points?<br><br><br>A) Propane has the most electrons, so the strongest London forces<br>B) Propane has only London forces, the ether adds dipole-dipole forces, and ethanol has hydrogen bonds<br>C) All three have hydrogen bonds, but ethanol has the most<br>D) Ethanol has the strongest covalent bonds<br>E) The ether has hydrogen bonds because it contains oxygen	<b>B) Propane has only London forces, the ether adds dipole-dipole forces, and ethanol has hydrogen bonds</b><br><br>The molar masses are almost the same, so London forces are similar. The ether is polar (C–O–C is bent): dipole-dipole forces. Ethanol has an O–H group, which forms hydrogen bonds. The ether has no H bonded to O, so it cannot <b>donate</b> a hydrogen bond.	ImatPath Chemistry practice
chem07-004	Which order lists the hydrogen halides from the lowest boiling point to the highest?<br><br><br>A) HCl < HBr < HI < HF<br>B) HF < HCl < HBr < HI<br>C) HI < HBr < HCl < HF<br>D) HCl < HF < HBr < HI<br>E) HF < HI < HBr < HCl	<b>A) HCl < HBr < HI < HF</b><br><br>From HCl to HI the number of electrons increases, so London forces and boiling points rise. HF is out of line: its hydrogen bonds are stronger than the London forces of HI, so it has the highest boiling point of the four.	ImatPath Chemistry practice
chem07-006	Which list shows the types of attraction from the weakest to the strongest (typical values)?<br><br><br>A) Hydrogen bond, London forces, dipole-dipole, ionic bond<br>B) Dipole-dipole, London forces, hydrogen bond, ionic bond<br>C) London forces, hydrogen bond, dipole-dipole, ionic bond<br>D) Ionic bond, hydrogen bond, dipole-dipole, London forces<br>E) London forces, dipole-dipole, hydrogen bond, ionic bond	<b>E) London forces, dipole-dipole, hydrogen bond, ionic bond</b><br><br>London forces are the weakest and exist between all particles. Dipole-dipole forces are stronger, hydrogen bonds are the strongest of the intermolecular forces, and an ionic bond is a bond inside a lattice and stronger still.	ImatPath Chemistry practice
chem07-008	Pentane (36 °C) and 2,2-dimethylpropane (neopentane, 9.5 °C) are isomers. Which explanation is correct?<br><br><br>A) Pentane forms hydrogen bonds, neopentane does not<br>B) Neopentane has a smaller molar mass<br>C) Pentane has stronger C–C bonds<br>D) The long, straight pentane molecules have a larger contact surface, so stronger London forces<br>E) Neopentane is polar, so it boils lower	<b>D) The long, straight pentane molecules have a larger contact surface, so stronger London forces</b><br><br>Both have the formula C<sub>5</sub>H<sub>12</sub>, so the same number of electrons. The compact, nearly spherical neopentane touches its neighbours at fewer points, so the total London attraction is lower.	ImatPath Chemistry practice
