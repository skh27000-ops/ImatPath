phys16-001	<b>Electric current</b>: what is it?	<b>Electric current</b> is the <b>rate of flow of electric charge</b>: <i>I = (Δ Q)/(Δ t)</i>. The unit is the <b>ampere (A)</b>: 1 A = 1 C/s.	ImatPath Physics definition
phys16-002	<b>Resistance</b>: what is it?	The <b>resistance</b> of a component is the potential difference across it divided by the current through it: <i>R = (V)/(I)</i>. The unit is the <b>ohm (<i>Ω</i>)</b>: 1 <i>Ω</i> = 1 V/A.	ImatPath Physics definition
phys16-007	<b>Kirchhoff's laws</b>: what is it?	<b>First law (junction rule):</b> the total current into a junction equals the total current out of it (<i>∑ I = 0</i>). This is <b>conservation of charge</b>.<br><b>Second law (loop rule):</b> around any closed loop, the sum of the emfs equals the sum of the potential drops, <i>∑ε = ∑ IR</i>. This is <b>conservation of energy</b>.	ImatPath Physics definition
phys16-019	<small>Series and parallel</small><br><b>Current</b><br><i>Series · Parallel?</i>	<b>Series:</b> <b>same</b> through each<br><b>Parallel:</b> <b>splits</b>: <i>I = I<sub>1</sub> + I<sub>2</sub></i>	ImatPath Physics recall
phys16-020	<small>Series and parallel</small><br><b>Voltage</b><br><i>Series · Parallel?</i>	<b>Series:</b> <b>splits</b>: <i>V = V<sub>1</sub> + V<sub>2</sub></i><br><b>Parallel:</b> <b>same</b> across each	ImatPath Physics recall
phys16-021	<small>Series and parallel</small><br><b>Total resistance</b><br><i>Series · Parallel?</i>	<b>Series:</b> <i>R = R<sub>1</sub> + R<sub>2</sub></i><br><b>Parallel:</b> <i>(1)/(R) = (1)/(R<sub>1</sub>) + (1)/(R<sub>2</sub>)</i>	ImatPath Physics recall

phys16-003	Formula to know: <b>Resistance and Ohm's law</b>	<i>V = IR</i>	ImatPath Physics formula
phys16-004	Formula to know: <b>Resistivity</b>	<i>R = ρ(L)/(A)</i>	ImatPath Physics formula

phys16-006	Formula to know: <b>Internal resistance</b>	<i>V = ε - Ir &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; ε = I(R + r)</i>	ImatPath Physics formula
phys16-016	Key points: <b>Definitions</b>	• <i>I = Δ Q/Δ t</i> (A). <i>V = W/Q</i> (V). <i>R = V/I</i> (<i>Ω</i>). Conventional current: + to −.<br>• Resistivity: <i>R = ρ L/A</i>. <i>ρ</i> in <i>Ω</i> m.	ImatPath Physics summary
phys16-017	Key points: <b>Circuits</b>	• Series: same <i>I</i>, <i>R = R<sub>1</sub> + R<sub>2</sub></i>. Parallel: same <i>V</i>, <i>1/R = 1/R<sub>1</sub> + 1/R<sub>2</sub></i>.<br>• Divider: <i>V<sub>2</sub> = VR<sub>2</sub>/(R<sub>1</sub> + R<sub>2</sub>)</i>. Ammeter in series (low R), voltmeter in parallel (high R).<br>• Internal resistance: <i>V = ε - Ir</i>.	ImatPath Physics summary
phys16-018	Key points: <b>Kirchhoff</b>	• Junction: <i>∑ I<sub>in</sub> = ∑ I<sub>out</sub></i> (charge). Loop: <i>∑ε = ∑ IR</i> (energy).	ImatPath Physics summary
phys16-008	A 12 V battery of internal resistance 0.50 <i>Ω</i> is connected to a 6.0 <i>Ω</i> and a 3.0 <i>Ω</i> resistor in parallel, which are in series with a 4.0 <i>Ω</i> resistor. What is the current in the 3.0 <i>Ω</i> resistor?<br><br><br>A) 1.85 A<br>B) 1.23 A<br>C) 0.615 A<br>D) 4 A<br>E) 2.46 A	<b>B) 1.23 A</b><br><br>Total resistance: <i>0.50 + 4.0 + (6 × 3)/9 = 6.5 Ω</i>. <i>I = 12/6.5 = 1.85</i> A. The parallel pair has <i>2.0 Ω</i>, so its voltage is <i>3.69</i> V. <i>I<sub>3</sub> = 3.69/3.0 = 1.23</i> A.	ImatPath Physics practice
phys16-010	A copper wire 50 m long and 1.0 mm in diameter (<i>ρ = 1.7 × 10<sup>-8</sup> Ω</i> m) carries a current of 10 A. What power is lost as heat in the wire?<br><br><br>A) 108 W<br>B) 433 W<br>C) 27.1 W<br>D) 10.8 W<br>E) 1.08 W	<b>A) 108 W</b><br><br>Area <i>= π(0.50 × 10<sup>-3</sup>)<sup>2</sup> = 7.85 × 10<sup>-7</sup></i> m<sup>2</sup>. <i>R = ρ L/A = 1.08 Ω</i>. <i>P = I<sup>2</sup>R = 100 × 1.08 = 108</i> W.	ImatPath Physics practice
phys16-012	A battery has emf 9.0 V and internal resistance 3.0 <i>Ω</i>. What is the maximum power it can deliver to an external resistor?<br><br><br>A) 27 W<br>B) 13.5 W<br>C) 3.4 W<br>D) 9.0 W<br>E) 6.75 W	<b>E) 6.75 W</b><br><br>Maximum power is transferred when <i>R = r = 3.0 Ω</i>. Then <i>I = 9.0/6.0 = 1.5</i> A and <i>P = I<sup>2</sup>R = 2.25 × 3.0 = 6.75</i> W.	ImatPath Physics practice
phys16-014	A galvanometer of resistance 100 <i>Ω</i> gives full-scale deflection for 1.0 mA. What shunt resistor must be connected in parallel to turn it into an ammeter with a full-scale reading of 1.0 A?<br><br><br>A) 100 <i>Ω</i><br>B) 0.001 <i>Ω</i><br>C) 10 <i>Ω</i><br>D) 0.10 <i>Ω</i><br>E) 1.0 <i>Ω</i>	<b>D) 0.10 <i>Ω</i></b><br><br>At full scale the galvanometer has <i>V = 100 × 0.001 = 0.10</i> V across it. The shunt carries <i>1.0 - 0.001 = 0.999</i> A at 0.10 V: <i>R<sub>s</sub> = 0.10/0.999 ≈ 0.10 Ω</i>.	ImatPath Physics practice
