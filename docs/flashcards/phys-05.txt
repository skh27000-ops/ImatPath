phys05-001	<b>Newton's first law</b>: what is it?	An object stays at <b>rest</b>, or moves at <b>constant velocity</b>, unless a <b>resultant (net) force</b> acts on it.	ImatPath Physics definition
phys05-002	<b>Newton's second law</b>: what is it?	The acceleration of an object is <b>proportional to the net force</b> and <b>inversely proportional to its mass</b>, in the direction of the net force.	ImatPath Physics definition
phys05-004	<b>Newton's third law</b>: what is it?	If body A exerts a force on body B, then B exerts a force on A that is <b>equal in size, opposite in direction and of the same type</b>.	ImatPath Physics definition
phys05-020	<small>Forces</small><br><b>Weight</b><br><i>Symbol · Direction · Notes?</i>	<b>Symbol:</b> <i>W = mg</i><br><b>Direction:</b> down, to Earth's centre<br><b>Notes:</b> the pull of gravity on a mass	ImatPath Physics recall
phys05-021	<small>Answers & explanations</small><br><b>1st</b>	no net force, constant velocity (or rest).	ImatPath Physics key idea
phys05-022	<small>Answers & explanations</small><br><b>2nd</b>	<i>F<sub>net</sub> = ma</i> (vector sum, each direction separately).	ImatPath Physics key idea
phys05-023	<small>Answers & explanations</small><br><b>3rd</b>	equal and opposite forces of the same type on <b>different</b> bodies.	ImatPath Physics key idea
phys05-024	<small>Answers & explanations</small><br><b>Terminal velocity</b>	drag equals weight, acceleration is zero.	ImatPath Physics key idea
phys05-003	Formula to know: <b>The second law</b>	<i>F<sub>net</sub> = ma</i>	ImatPath Physics formula
phys05-005	Formula to know: <b>Friction</b>	<i>f ≤ μ<sub>s</sub> N &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;</i> (static, object not moving) <i>&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; f = μ<sub>k</sub> N &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;</i> (kinetic, sliding)	ImatPath Physics formula
phys05-006	Formula to know: <b>The inclined plane</b>	along the slope: <i>mgsinθ</i><br>perpendicular to the slope: <i>mgcosθ</i><br>normal force: <i>N = mgcosθ</i>	ImatPath Physics formula
phys05-007	Formula to know: <b>Springs and Hooke's law</b>	<i>F = kx</i>	ImatPath Physics formula
phys05-008	Formula to know: <b>Newton's law of gravitation</b>	<i>F = G(m<sub>1</sub> m<sub>2</sub>)/(r<sup>2</sup>) &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; G = 6.67 × 10<sup>-11</sup> N m<sup>2</sup>/kg<sup>2</sup></i>	ImatPath Physics formula
phys05-017	Key points: <b>Newton's laws</b>	• <b>1st</b>: no net force, constant velocity (or rest).<br>• <b>2nd</b>: <i>F<sub>net</sub> = ma</i> (vector sum, each direction separately).<br>• <b>3rd</b>: equal and opposite forces of the same type on <b>different</b> bodies.	ImatPath Physics summary
phys05-018	Key points: <b>Common forces</b>	• Weight <i>W = mg</i>. Friction <i>f = μ N</i>. Hooke <i>F = kx</i>.<br>• Slope: along <i>mgsinθ</i>, perpendicular <i>N = mgcosθ</i>.<br>• Gravitation <i>F = Gm<sub>1</sub>m<sub>2</sub>/r<sup>2</sup></i>.	ImatPath Physics summary
phys05-019	Key points: <b>Equilibrium</b>	• Net force zero: <i>∑ F<sub>x</sub> = 0</i>, <i>∑ F<sub>y</sub> = 0</i>.<br>• <b>Terminal velocity</b>: drag equals weight, acceleration is zero.	ImatPath Physics summary
phys05-009	A 3.0 kg block is tied by a light string to a 5.0 kg block on a frictionless table. A horizontal force of 24 N pulls the 5.0 kg block, with the 3.0 kg block behind it. What is the tension in the string?<br><br><br>A) 24 N<br>B) 9.0 N<br>C) 15 N<br>D) 14.4 N<br>E) 3.0 N	<b>B) 9.0 N</b><br><br>Treat both blocks together: <i>a = 24/8.0 = 3.0</i> m/s<sup>2</sup>. The tension is the only horizontal force on the 3.0 kg block: <i>T = 3.0 × 3.0 = 9.0</i> N.	ImatPath Physics practice
phys05-011	A 5.0 kg block slides from rest down a 30° slope. The coefficient of kinetic friction is 0.30 (<i>g = 9.8</i> m/s<sup>2</sup>). What is its speed after it has slid 2.0 m?<br><br><br>A) 3.07 m/s<br>B) 4.43 m/s<br>C) 3.43 m/s<br>D) 5.46 m/s<br>E) 4.71 m/s	<b>A) 3.07 m/s</b><br><br>Along the slope: <i>a = g(sin 30<sup>°</sup> - μcos 30<sup>°</sup>) = 9.8(0.500 - 0.260) = 2.35</i> m/s<sup>2</sup>. Then <i>v = √(2as) = √(2(2.35)(2.0)) = 3.07</i> m/s.	ImatPath Physics practice
phys05-013	A 2.0 kg block is pulled at <b>constant speed</b> across a rough floor (<i>μ<sub>k</sub> = 0.25</i>, <i>g = 9.8</i> m/s<sup>2</sup>) by a horizontal spring of constant 200 N/m. How far is the spring stretched?<br><br><br>A) 10.2 cm<br>B) 9.8 cm<br>C) 4.9 cm<br>D) 40.8 cm<br>E) 2.45 cm	<b>E) 2.45 cm</b><br><br>Constant speed means zero net force, so the spring force equals friction: <i>kx = μ mg = 0.25 × 2.0 × 9.8 = 4.9</i> N. <i>x = 4.9/200 = 0.0245</i> m <i>= 2.5</i> cm.	ImatPath Physics practice
phys05-015	A 20 kg sign hangs from two cables attached to a ceiling. One cable makes 40° with the horizontal and the other 30° with the horizontal, on opposite sides. What is the tension in the cable at 40°? (<i>g = 9.8</i> m/s<sup>2</sup>)<br><br><br>A) 160 N<br>B) 196 N<br>C) 152 N<br>D) 181 N<br>E) 305 N	<b>D) 181 N</b><br><br>Horizontal balance: <i>T<sub>1</sub>cos 40<sup>circ</sup> = T<sub>2</sub>cos 30<sup>circ</sup></i>, so <i>T<sub>2</sub> = 0.885T<sub>1</sub></i>. Vertical balance: <i>T<sub>1</sub>sin 40<sup>circ</sup> + T<sub>2</sub>sin 30<sup>circ</sup> = 196</i> N, so <i>T<sub>1</sub>(0.643 + 0.442) = 196</i> and <i>T<sub>1</sub> = 181</i> N (and <i>T<sub>2</sub> = 160</i> N).	ImatPath Physics practice
