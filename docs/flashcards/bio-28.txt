bio28-022	<small>Connective tissue</small><br><b>Adipose (fat)</b><br><i>Matrix · Cells · Role and example?</i>	<b>Matrix:</b> Little matrix<br><b>Cells:</b> <b>Adipocytes</b> full of fat<br><b>Role and example:</b> Energy storage, insulation, cushioning	ImatPath Biology recall
bio28-023	<small>Connective tissue</small><br><b>Cartilage</b><br><i>Matrix · Cells · Role and example?</i>	<b>Matrix:</b> Firm, rubbery <b>collagen</b> with <b>chondroitin sulfate</b>, <b>no blood supply</b><br><b>Cells:</b> <b>Chondrocytes</b> in spaces<br><b>Role and example:</b> Joints, nose, ear, trachea rings. bio28-025	<small>Connective tissue</small><br><b>Blood</b><br><i>Matrix · Cells · Role and example?</i>	<b>Matrix:</b> Liquid <b>plasma</b><br><b>Cells:</b> Red cells, white cells, platelets<br><b>Role and example:</b> Transport, defence, clotting	ImatPath Biology recall
bio28-026	<small>Muscle tissue</small><br><b>Location</b><br><i>Skeletal · Cardiac · Smooth?</i>	<b>Skeletal:</b> Attached to bones<br><b>Cardiac:</b> Heart wall<br><b>Smooth:</b> Walls of the gut, blood vessels, bladder, uterus	ImatPath Biology recall

bio28-029	<small>Muscle tissue</small><br><b>Control</b><br><i>Skeletal · Cardiac · Smooth?</i>	<b>Skeletal:</b> <b>Voluntary</b><br><b>Cardiac:</b> <b>Involuntary</b><br><b>Smooth:</b> <b>Involuntary</b>	ImatPath Biology recall
bio28-030	<small>Muscle tissue</small><br><b>Contraction</b><br><i>Skeletal · Cardiac · Smooth?</i>	<b>Skeletal:</b> Fast, tires easily<br><b>Cardiac:</b> Rhythmic, <b>never tires</b><br><b>Smooth:</b> Slow, sustained	ImatPath Biology recall
bio28-031	<small>Muscle tissue</small><br><b>Special features</b><br><i>Skeletal · Cardiac · Smooth?</i>	<b>Skeletal:</b> Very long fibres<br><b>Cardiac:</b> <b>Intercalated discs</b> with gap junctions that pass the signal<br><b>Smooth:</b> Spindle-shaped cells	ImatPath Biology recall
bio28-009	Key points: <b>Organisation</b>	• Cell → tissue → organ → organ system → organism. Four basic tissues.	ImatPath Biology summary
bio28-010	Key points: <b>Epithelial</b>	• Tightly packed, basement membrane, no blood vessels. Squamous, cuboidal, columnar × simple, stratified, pseudostratified. Skin = stratified squamous.	ImatPath Biology summary
bio28-011	Key points: <b>Connective</b>	• Few cells in a large matrix (collagen, elastin). Loose, dense (<b>tendon, ligament</b>), adipose, cartilage, bone, blood.	ImatPath Biology summary
bio28-012	Key points: <b>Muscle</b>	• <b>Skeletal</b> (striated, voluntary, many nuclei), <b>cardiac</b> (striated, branched, intercalated discs), <b>smooth</b> (non-striated, involuntary).	ImatPath Biology summary
bio28-013	Key points: <b>Nervous</b>	• <b>Neurons</b> (dendrites, cell body, axon, myelin) and <b>glia</b>.	ImatPath Biology summary
bio28-001	Which muscle type has branched cells joined by intercalated discs with gap junctions?<br><br><br>A) Skeletal muscle<br>B) Cardiac muscle<br>C) Smooth muscle<br>D) Both skeletal and smooth muscle<br>E) None, because muscle is not a tissue	<b>B) Cardiac muscle</b><br><br>Cardiac muscle cells are branched, striated and joined end to end by intercalated discs that allow the electrical signal to pass quickly, so the heart contracts as a unit.	ImatPath Biology practice
bio28-003	In multiple sclerosis the immune system destroys the myelin sheath around axons of the central nervous system. What is the main effect?<br><br><br>A) Nerve impulses are conducted more slowly or are blocked<br>B) The impulses are faster<br>C) The neurons make more neurotransmitter<br>D) The cell bodies become muscle cells<br>E) Blood flow to the brain stops	<b>A) Nerve impulses are conducted more slowly or are blocked</b><br><br>Myelin insulates axons and allows saltatory conduction (jumping from node to node). Without it, the impulse slows or fails, causing weakness and loss of coordination.	ImatPath Biology practice
bio28-005	Which structure allows the muscle cells of the heart to contract almost simultaneously?<br><br><br>A) Tight junctions<br>B) Desmosomes only<br>C) Hemidesmosomes<br>D) Plasmodesmata<br>E) Gap junctions in the intercalated discs	<b>E) Gap junctions in the intercalated discs</b><br><br>Gap junctions are channels that let ions pass between cells, so the action potential spreads quickly. Desmosomes in the same discs hold the cells together against the force of contraction.	ImatPath Biology practice
bio28-007	Which structure joins a skeletal muscle to a bone, and what is it made of?<br><br><br>A) A ligament, made of smooth muscle<br>B) A tendon, made of simple squamous epithelium<br>C) A ligament, made of bone<br>D) A tendon, made of dense regular connective tissue rich in collagen<br>E) A nerve, made of cartilage	<b>D) A tendon, made of dense regular connective tissue rich in collagen</b><br><br>Tendons connect muscle to bone and ligaments connect bone to bone. Both are dense regular connective tissue with parallel collagen fibres to resist pulling in one direction.	ImatPath Biology practice
