chem02-020	Key points: <b>The gas laws</b>	• <b>Always use kelvin</b>: <i>T(K) = θ(<sup>°</sup>C) + 273</i>.<br>• Combined law: <i>P<sub>1</sub>V<sub>1</sub>/T<sub>1</sub> = P<sub>2</sub>V<sub>2</sub>/T<sub>2</sub></i>.<br>• Ideal gas equation: <i>PV = nRT</i>. <i>R = 0.0821</i> L atm/(mol K) or 8.31 J/(mol K).	ImatPath Chemistry summary
chem02-021	Key points: <b>Useful values</b>	• Molar volume at STP: <b>22.4 L/mol</b> (0 °C, 1 atm).<br>• <i>M = mRT/PV</i> and <i>ρ = PM/RT</i>: bigger M means denser gas.<br>• 1 atm = 101.3 kPa = 760 mmHg.	ImatPath Chemistry summary
chem02-022	Key points: <b>Mixtures and real gases</b>	• Dalton: <i>P<sub>total</sub></i> is the sum of partial pressures. <i>P<sub>A</sub> = x<sub>A</sub> P<sub>total</sub></i>.<br>• Real gases deviate at <b>high pressure and low temperature</b>.	ImatPath Chemistry summary
chem02-012	A gas occupies 2.0 L at 1.0 atm and 27 °C. What volume does it occupy at 4.0 atm and 127 °C?<br><br><br>A) 10.7 L<br>B) 0.667 L<br>C) 2.35 L<br>D) 0.5 L<br>E) 6 L	<b>B) 0.667 L</b><br><br>Use <i>P<sub>1</sub>V<sub>1</sub>/T<sub>1</sub> = P<sub>2</sub>V<sub>2</sub>/T<sub>2</sub></i> with kelvin: <i>T<sub>1</sub> = 300</i> K, <i>T<sub>2</sub> = 400</i> K. <i>V<sub>2</sub> = 2.0 × (1.0/4.0) × (400/300) = 0.667</i> L.	ImatPath Chemistry practice
chem02-014	A 10 L vessel at 27 °C contains 2.0 mol of N<sub>2</sub> and 3.0 mol of O<sub>2</sub>. What is the partial pressure of N<sub>2</sub>? (<i>R = 0.0821</i> L atm/(mol K))<br><br><br>A) 4.93 atm<br>B) 12.3 atm<br>C) 7.39 atm<br>D) 49.3 atm<br>E) 2.46 atm	<b>A) 4.93 atm</b><br><br>Total <i>n = 5.0</i> mol, so <i>P<sub>total</sub> = nRT/V = 5.0 × 0.0821 × 300/10 = 12.3</i> atm. N<sub>2</sub> is 2.0/5.0 = 0.40 of the moles, so <i>P<sub>N<sub>2</sub></sub> = 0.40 × 12.3 = 4.93</i> atm.	ImatPath Chemistry practice
chem02-016	A 2.0 L flask holds a gas at 3.0 atm. It is connected by a tap to a 3.0 L flask holding the same gas at 1.0 atm. The temperature is constant. What is the final pressure after the tap is opened?<br><br><br>A) 2.0 atm<br>B) 1.0 atm<br>C) 4.0 atm<br>D) 1 atm<br>E) 1.8 atm	<b>E) 1.8 atm</b><br><br>Boyle: the product <i>PV</i> for each flask is the amount of gas. Total <i>PV = 2.0 × 3.0 + 3.0 × 1.0 = 9.0</i> L atm. The total volume is 5.0 L, so <i>P = 9.0/5.0 = 1.8</i> atm.	ImatPath Chemistry practice
chem02-018	A balloon of volume 5.0 L is released at 1.00 atm and 25 °C. It rises to a height where the pressure is 0.50 atm and the temperature is <i>-23</i> °C. What is its new volume?<br><br><br>A) 11.9 L<br>B) 10 L<br>C) -9.2 L<br>D) 8.39 L<br>E) 2.1 L	<b>D) 8.39 L</b><br><br><i>T<sub>1</sub> = 298</i> K and <i>T<sub>2</sub> = 250</i> K. <i>V<sub>2</sub> = V<sub>1</sub> × (P<sub>1</sub>/P<sub>2</sub>) × (T<sub>2</sub>/T<sub>1</sub>) = 5.0 × 2.0 × (250/298) = 8.39</i> L. Lower pressure expands the gas, lower temperature shrinks it.	ImatPath Chemistry practice
