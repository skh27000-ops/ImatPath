bio10-014	<b>enzyme</b>: what is it?	An <b>enzyme</b> is a <b>protein that speeds up a chemical reaction</b> without being used up.	ImatPath Biology term
bio10-012	<small>Controlling enzymes</small><br><b>Competitive</b><br><i>Where it binds · Effect · Can more substrate overcome it??</i>	<b>Where it binds:</b> <b>Active site</b>, because it looks like the substrate<br><b>Effect:</b> Blocks the substrate from binding<br><b>Can more substrate overcome it?:</b> <b>Yes</b>: more substrate outcompetes it	ImatPath Biology recall
bio10-013	<small>Controlling enzymes</small><br><b>Non-competitive</b><br><i>Where it binds · Effect · Can more substrate overcome it??</i>	<b>Where it binds:</b> <b>Another site</b> (allosteric site)<br><b>Effect:</b> Changes the shape of the active site, so it no longer works<br><b>Can more substrate overcome it?:</b> <b>No</b>	ImatPath Biology recall
bio10-015	<small>What enzymes do</small><br><b>Lock and key model</b>	the substrate fits the active site exactly, like a key in a lock.	ImatPath Biology key idea
bio10-016	<small>What enzymes do</small><br><b>Induced fit model</b>	the active site is slightly flexible and <b>changes shape</b> to grip the substrate more tightly once it binds. This is the model accepted today.	ImatPath Biology key idea
bio10-017	<small>Answers & explanations</small><br><b>Temperature</b>	optimum, then denatures. <b>pH</b>: optimum for each enzyme. <b>Substrate</b>: rises then plateaus (<b>V<sub>max</sub></b>). <b>Enzyme</b>: rises in proportion.	ImatPath Biology key idea
bio10-018	<small>Answers & explanations</small><br><b>Feedback inhibition</b>	the end product switches off an early enzyme.	ImatPath Biology key idea
bio10-009	Key points: <b>How enzymes work</b>	• Protein catalysts that <b>lower activation energy</b>. Not used up. Specific active site (<b>induced fit</b>).	ImatPath Biology summary
bio10-010	Key points: <b>Rate factors</b>	• <b>Temperature</b>: optimum, then denatures. <b>pH</b>: optimum for each enzyme. <b>Substrate</b>: rises then plateaus (<b>V<sub>max</sub></b>). <b>Enzyme</b>: rises in proportion.	ImatPath Biology summary
bio10-011	Key points: <b>Control</b>	• <b>Competitive</b> inhibitor: active site, overcome by more substrate. <b>Non-competitive</b>: allosteric site, not overcome.<br>• <b>Feedback inhibition</b>: the end product switches off an early enzyme.<br>• <b>Cofactors</b> (ions) and <b>coenzymes</b> (organic, e.g. NAD<sup>+</sup>).	ImatPath Biology summary
bio10-001	An enzyme reaction has reached its maximum rate because all the active sites are occupied. How can the rate be increased?<br><br><br>A) By adding more substrate<br>B) By adding more enzyme<br>C) By adding a competitive inhibitor<br>D) By adding more product<br>E) By lowering the temperature	<b>B) By adding more enzyme</b><br><br>At saturation the number of enzyme molecules is limiting, so adding substrate has no effect. More enzyme means more active sites, so the maximum rate rises in proportion.	ImatPath Biology practice
bio10-003	An enzyme-catalysed reaction doubles in rate for every 10 °C rise (up to its optimum). The rate at 20 °C is 4 units. What is the rate at 40 °C?<br><br><br>A) 16 units<br>B) 8 units<br>C) 12 units<br>D) 32 units<br>E) 80 units	<b>A) 16 units</b><br><br>From 20 °C to 40 °C there are two steps of 10 °C, so the rate doubles twice: <i>4 × 2 × 2 = 16</i> units (provided the enzyme is not denatured).	ImatPath Biology practice
bio10-005	In a metabolic pathway A → B → C → D, product D binds to a site on enzyme 1 and reduces its activity. What does this show?<br><br><br>A) Competitive inhibition at the active site<br>B) Denaturation by heat<br>C) A rise in substrate concentration<br>D) The action of a coenzyme<br>E) Feedback (end-product) inhibition by allosteric regulation	<b>E) Feedback (end-product) inhibition by allosteric regulation</b><br><br>When D builds up, it binds to an allosteric site of the first enzyme, changing its shape and slowing the whole pathway. This stops the cell from making more product than needed.	ImatPath Biology practice
bio10-007	Which of these is a coenzyme?<br><br><br>A) Mg<sup>2+</sup><br>B) Zn<sup>2+</sup><br>C) ATP synthase<br>D) NAD<sup>+</sup> (made from the vitamin niacin)<br>E) Haemoglobin	<b>D) NAD<sup>+</sup> (made from the vitamin niacin)</b><br><br>Coenzymes are organic molecules, often derived from vitamins, that carry chemical groups or electrons between enzymes. Mg<sup>2+</sup> and Zn<sup>2+</sup> are inorganic cofactors (metal ions).	ImatPath Biology practice
