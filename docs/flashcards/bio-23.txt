bio23-017	<small>Autosomal recessive diseases</small><br><b>Gene</b>	<i>CFTR</i> on chromosome 7. It codes for a <b>chloride-ion channel</b> in the membranes of epithelial cells. The most common mutation, <b>ΔF508</b>, is the <b>deletion of one amino acid (phenylalanine)</b>, so the protein misfolds and does not reach the membrane.	ImatPath Biology key idea
bio23-018	<small>Autosomal recessive diseases</small><br><b>Effect</b>	chloride cannot leave the cell, so less water follows by osmosis, and <b>thick, sticky mucus</b> builds up in the lungs, pancreas and intestines. The result is chest infections, poor digestion and malnutrition.	ImatPath Biology key idea
bio23-019	<small>Autosomal recessive diseases</small><br><b>Frequency</b>	about 1 in 2,500 among people of European descent. Carriers: about <b>1 in 25</b>.	ImatPath Biology key idea
bio23-020	<small>Autosomal recessive diseases</small><br><b>Treatment</b>	physiotherapy, antibiotics, pancreatic enzymes, and modern drugs that help the faulty channel work.	ImatPath Biology key idea
bio23-021	<small>Autosomal recessive diseases</small><br><b>Heterozygote advantage</b>	carriers (<b>HbA/HbS</b>) are <b>more resistant to malaria</b>. This keeps the allele common in regions where malaria occurs (Africa, parts of the Mediterranean and India). It is a classic example of <b>balanced polymorphism</b>.	ImatPath Biology key idea
bio23-022	<small>Autosomal dominant diseases</small><br><b>Risk</b>	50% for a child of an affected parent. The more repeats, the earlier the onset (<b>anticipation</b>). There is no cure, but a predictive test exists.	ImatPath Biology key idea
bio23-023	<small>Answers & explanations</small><br><b>CF</b>	CFTR chloride channel (ΔF508), thick mucus. <b>Sickle cell</b>: Glu → Val, HbS, <b>heterozygote advantage vs malaria</b>. <b>PKU</b>: phenylalanine hydroxylase, treat by diet. <b>Tay-Sachs</b>: hexosaminidase A.	ImatPath Biology key idea

bio23-003	Why do children with phenylketonuria (PKU) who start a low-phenylalanine diet shortly after birth develop normally?<br><br><br>A) The diet prevents toxic phenylalanine and its by-products from building up, because the enzyme is missing<br>B) The diet replaces the faulty gene<br>C) The diet makes the missing enzyme<br>D) Phenylalanine is not toxic<br>E) The diet lowers the pH of the blood	<b>A) The diet prevents toxic phenylalanine and its by-products from building up, because the enzyme is missing</b><br><br>PKU is caused by a defect in phenylalanine hydroxylase. Phenylalanine accumulates and damages the developing brain. Newborn screening and a diet low in this amino acid avoid the damage even though the gene defect remains.	ImatPath Biology practice
bio23-005	A heterozygous person with Huntington disease (autosomal dominant) and an unaffected partner have two children. What is the probability that both children are unaffected?<br><br><br>A) 1/2<br>B) 1/16<br>C) 3/4<br>D) 1/8<br>E) 1/4	<b>E) 1/4</b><br><br>A heterozygous parent passes the allele to each child with probability 1/2, so each child is unaffected with probability 1/2. For two children: <i>1/2 × 1/2 = 1/4</i>.	ImatPath Biology practice
bio23-007	In familial hypercholesterolaemia caused by LDL receptor defects, heterozygotes have about half the normal number of functional receptors. What is the likely consequence?<br><br><br>A) Blood cholesterol is very low<br>B) Cells make less cholesterol<br>C) Nothing changes in the blood<br>D) Blood LDL cholesterol is raised and the risk of early heart disease increases<br>E) LDL is destroyed by the kidneys	<b>D) Blood LDL cholesterol is raised and the risk of early heart disease increases</b><br><br>Fewer receptors mean less LDL is taken up by cells, so more stays in the blood and deposits in artery walls (atherosclerosis). Homozygotes have almost no receptors and are much more severely affected.	ImatPath Biology practice
