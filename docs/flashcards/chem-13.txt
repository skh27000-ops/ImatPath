chem13-002	<b>Dynamic equilibrium</b>: what is it?	A <b>dynamic equilibrium</b> is the state of a reversible reaction in a <b>closed system</b> when the <b>forward and reverse reaction rates are equal</b>. The <b>concentrations stay constant</b>, although both reactions are still going on.	ImatPath Chemistry definition
chem13-004	<b>Le Chatelier's principle</b>: what is it?	<b>Le Chatelier's principle</b>: if a system at equilibrium is <b>disturbed</b>, the equilibrium <b>shifts to oppose the change</b>.	ImatPath Chemistry definition
chem13-025	<b>solubility product</b>: what is it?	The <b>solubility product</b> <i>K<sub>sp</sub></i> leaves out the solid.	ImatPath Chemistry term
chem13-017	<small>The equilibrium constant</small><br><b>Very large (over about <i>10<sup>3</sup></i>)</b><br><i>Meaning?</i>	Equilibrium lies to the <b>right</b>: mostly products	ImatPath Chemistry recall
chem13-018	<small>The equilibrium constant</small><br><b>Around 1</b><br><i>Meaning?</i>	Significant amounts of both	ImatPath Chemistry recall
chem13-019	<small>The equilibrium constant</small><br><b>Very small (below about <i>10<sup>-3</sup></i>)</b><br><i>Meaning?</i>	Equilibrium lies to the <b>left</b>: mostly reactants	ImatPath Chemistry recall

chem13-003	Formula to know: <b>The equilibrium constant</b>	<i>K<sub>c</sub> = ([C]<sup>c</sup>   [D]<sup>d</sup>)/([A]<sup>a</sup>   [B]<sup>b</sup>)</i>	ImatPath Chemistry formula

chem13-006	Formula to know: <b>Sparingly soluble salts</b>	AgCl(s) ⇌ Ag<sup>+</sup>(aq) + Cl<sup>-</sup>(aq)        <i>K<sub>sp</sub> = [Ag<sup>+</sup>][Cl<sup>-</sup>]</i>	ImatPath Chemistry formula
chem13-015	Key points: <b>Equilibrium</b>	• Reversible, <b>closed system</b>, forward rate = reverse rate. Concentrations <b>constant</b>, not equal.<br>• <i>K<sub>c</sub> = [products]<sup>coeff</sup> / [reactants]<sup>coeff</sup></i>. Leave out solids and pure liquids.<br>• Large <i>K<sub>c</sub></i>: mostly products. Small <i>K<sub>c</sub></i>: mostly reactants. <i>K<sub>c</sub></i> changes <b>only with temperature</b>.	ImatPath Chemistry summary
chem13-016	Key points: <b>Water and salts</b>	• <i>K<sub>w</sub> = [H<sup>+</sup>][OH<sup>-</sup>] = 10<sup>-14</sup></i> at 25 °C. Pure water: both <i>10<sup>-7</sup></i>.<br>• <i>K<sub>sp</sub></i>: product of the ion concentrations (solid left out). Precipitate if ionic product <i>> K<sub>sp</sub></i>.<br>• Common ion lowers solubility.	ImatPath Chemistry summary
chem13-007	0.50 mol/L of N<sub>2</sub>O<sub>4</sub> is placed in a vessel: N<sub>2</sub>O<sub>4</sub>(g) ⇌ 2NO<sub>2</sub>(g). At equilibrium <i>[NO<sub>2</sub>] = 0.20</i> mol/L. What is <i>K<sub>c</sub></i>?<br><br><br>A) 0.080<br>B) 0.10<br>C) 0.40<br>D) 0.040<br>E) 0.20	<b>B) 0.10</b><br><br>NO<sub>2</sub> formed is 0.20 mol/L, which uses 0.10 mol/L of N<sub>2</sub>O<sub>4</sub>. So <i>[N<sub>2</sub>O<sub>4</sub>] = 0.50 - 0.10 = 0.40</i> mol/L. <i>K<sub>c</sub> = [NO<sub>2</sub>]<sup>2</sup>/[N<sub>2</sub>O<sub>4</sub>] = 0.04/0.40 = 0.10</i>.	ImatPath Chemistry practice
chem13-009	For A + B ⇌ C, <i>K<sub>c</sub> = 4.0</i>. A mixture has <i>[A] = 0.50</i>, <i>[B] = 0.50</i> and <i>[C] = 0.60</i> mol/L. What is <i>Q</i> and which way does the reaction go?<br><br><br>A) <i>Q = 2.4</i>, towards C<br>B) <i>Q = 2.4</i>, towards A and B<br>C) <i>Q = 0.42</i>, towards C<br>D) <i>Q = 4.0</i>, no change<br>E) <i>Q = 1.2</i>, towards A and B	<b>A) <i>Q = 2.4</i>, towards C</b><br><br><i>Q = [C]/([A][B]) = 0.60/0.25 = 2.4</i>. Since <i>Q < K<sub>c</sub></i> there is too little product, so the reaction moves to the right (towards C) until <i>Q = K<sub>c</sub></i>.	ImatPath Chemistry practice
chem13-011	<i>K<sub>sp</sub></i> of AgCl is <i>1.8 × 10<sup>-10</sup></i>. What is its solubility in 0.10 mol/L NaCl?<br><br><br>A) 1.34 × 10<sup>-5</sup> mol/L<br>B) 1.8 × 10<sup>-10</sup> mol/L<br>C) 0.1 mol/L<br>D) 1.8 × 10<sup>-11</sup> mol/L<br>E) 1.8 × 10<sup>-9</sup> mol/L	<b>E) 1.8 × 10<sup>-9</sup> mol/L</b><br><br><i>K<sub>sp</sub> = [Ag<sup>+</sup>][Cl<sup>-</sup>]</i>. The chloride from NaCl (0.10 mol/L) is far more than the chloride from AgCl, so <i>[Cl<sup>-</sup>] ≈ 0.10</i>. <i>s = [Ag<sup>+</sup>] = 1.8 × 10<sup>-10</sup>/0.10 = 1.8 × 10<sup>-9</sup></i> mol/L. In pure water it would be <i>1.3 × 10<sup>-5</sup></i>: the <b>common ion effect</b>.	ImatPath Chemistry practice
chem13-013	50 cm<sup>3</sup> of <i>1.0 × 10<sup>-4</sup></i> mol/L AgNO<sub>3</sub> are mixed with 50 cm<sup>3</sup> of <i>1.0 × 10<sup>-4</sup></i> mol/L NaCl (<i>K<sub>sp</sub></i> of AgCl <i>= 1.8 × 10<sup>-10</sup></i>). What is the ion product and does a precipitate form?<br><br><br>A) <i>2.5 × 10<sup>-9</sup></i>, no<br>B) <i>1.0 × 10<sup>-8</sup></i>, no<br>C) <i>5.0 × 10<sup>-5</sup></i>, no<br>D) <i>2.5 × 10<sup>-9</sup></i>, yes<br>E) <i>1.0 × 10<sup>-8</sup></i>, yes	<b>D) <i>2.5 × 10<sup>-9</sup></i>, yes</b><br><br>Mixing doubles the volume, so each concentration halves to <i>5.0 × 10<sup>-5</sup></i> mol/L. <i>Q = (5.0 × 10<sup>-5</sup>)<sup>2</sup> = 2.5 × 10<sup>-9</sup></i>. Since <i>Q > K<sub>sp</sub></i> (<i>1.8 × 10<sup>-10</sup></i>), AgCl precipitates.	ImatPath Chemistry practice
