Incomplete: CO or C.<br>• <b>Free radical substitution</b> with Cl<sub>2</sub> or Br<sub>2</sub> in <b>UV</b>: initiation, propagation, termination.<br>• <b>Cracking</b>: long alkane → shorter alkane + alkene.	ImatPath Chemistry summary
chem20-015	Key points: <b>Alkenes</b>	• <b>Addition</b>: H<sub>2</sub>/Ni (alkane), Br<sub>2</sub> (dibromo), HBr (bromo, Markovnikov), steam/H<sub>3</sub>PO<sub>4</sub> (alcohol).<br>• Test: decolourises <b>bromine water</b>.<br>• Addition polymers: poly(ethene), PVC, polystyrene. Not biodegradable.	ImatPath Chemistry summary
chem20-016	Key points: <b>Benzene</b>	• Planar, <b>delocalised</b> ring, equal bond lengths, very stable.<br>• <b>Substitution</b>: nitration (HNO<sub>3</sub> + H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub>), bromination (Br<sub>2</sub> + FeBr<sub>3</sub>). No decolourisation of bromine water.	ImatPath Chemistry summary
chem20-005	10 cm<sup>3</sup> of propane are mixed with 60 cm<sup>3</sup> of oxygen and ignited. The mixture is cooled to room temperature (water is a liquid). What volume of gas remains? All volumes are measured at the same temperature and pressure.<br><br><br>A) 30 cm<sup>3</sup><br>B) 40 cm<sup>3</sup><br>C) 70 cm<sup>3</sup><br>D) 50 cm<sup>3</sup><br>E) 10 cm<sup>3</sup>	<b>B) 40 cm<sup>3</sup></b><br><br>C<sub>3</sub>H<sub>8</sub> + 5O<sub>2</sub> → 3CO<sub>2</sub> + 4H<sub>2</sub>O(l). 10 cm<sup>3</sup> of propane use 50 cm<sup>3</sup> of O<sub>2</sub> and give 30 cm<sup>3</sup> of CO<sub>2</sub>. Oxygen left: <i>60 - 50 = 10</i> cm<sup>3</sup>. Gas remaining: <i>30 + 10 = 40</i> cm<sup>3</sup>.	ImatPath Chemistry practice
chem20-007	0.10 mol of ethyne, C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>2</sub>, reacts with an excess of bromine. What is the mass of the product? (<i>A<sub>r</sub></i>: C 12, H 1, Br 80)<br><br><br>A) 34.6 g<br>B) 18.8 g<br>C) 16.0 g<br>D) 17.3 g<br>E) 69.2 g	<b>A) 34.6 g</b><br><br>Two Br<sub>2</sub> add across the triple bond: C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>2</sub> + 2Br<sub>2</sub> → CHBr<sub>2</sub>CHBr<sub>2</sub>. <i>M = 24 + 2 + 4 × 80 = 346</i> g/mol. Mass <i>= 0.10 × 346 = 34.6</i> g. (18.8 g would be the mass of 1,2-dibromoethane, from only one Br<sub>2</sub>.)	ImatPath Chemistry practice
chem20-009	The enthalpy of hydrogenation of cyclohexene is −120 kJ/mol and that of benzene is −208 kJ/mol. How much more stable is benzene than a hypothetical cyclohexa-1,3,5-triene with three separate C=C bonds?<br><br><br>A) 88 kJ/mol<br>B) 120 kJ/mol<br>C) 328 kJ/mol<br>D) 360 kJ/mol<br>E) 152 kJ/mol	<b>E) 152 kJ/mol</b><br><br>Three isolated double bonds would release <i>3 × (-120) = -360</i> kJ/mol on hydrogenation. Benzene releases only <i>-208</i> kJ/mol, so it starts <i>360 - 208 = 152</i> kJ/mol lower in energy. This is the stabilisation from delocalised electrons.	ImatPath Chemistry practice

Number from the end that gives the double bond the lowest number: C=C starts at C2. The methyl group is on C3: 3-methylpent-2-ene. (Numbering from the other end would put the double bond at 3.)
