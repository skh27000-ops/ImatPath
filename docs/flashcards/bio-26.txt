bio26-019	<b>natural selection</b>: what is it?	<b>Charles Darwin</b> (and independently Alfred Russel Wallace) proposed that species change over time by <b>natural selection</b>.	ImatPath Biology term
bio26-012	<small>Natural selection</small><br><b>Directional</b><br><i>What is favoured · Result · Example?</i>	<b>What is favoured:</b> <b>One extreme</b><br><b>Result:</b> The population <b>shifts</b> towards that extreme<br><b>Example:</b> Antibiotic resistance. Peppered moths becoming dark in polluted forests	ImatPath Biology recall
bio26-013	<small>Natural selection</small><br><b>Stabilising</b><br><i>What is favoured · Result · Example?</i>	<b>What is favoured:</b> The <b>average</b><br><b>Result:</b> Extremes are removed. <b>Variation shrinks</b><br><b>Example:</b> Human <b>birth weight</b>: very small and very large babies have higher mortality	ImatPath Biology recall
bio26-014	<small>Natural selection</small><br><b>Disruptive</b><br><i>What is favoured · Result · Example?</i>	<b>What is favoured:</b> <b>Both extremes</b><br><b>Result:</b> The population may <b>split in two</b><br><b>Example:</b> Finch beak sizes with only large and small seeds available	ImatPath Biology recall

bio26-020	<small>Natural selection</small><br><b>Variation</b>	individuals in a population differ in their traits.	ImatPath Biology key idea
bio26-021	<small>Natural selection</small><br><b>Heritability</b>	some of that variation is <b>inherited</b>, passed from parents to offspring.	ImatPath Biology key idea
bio26-022	<small>Natural selection</small><br><b>Overproduction</b>	more offspring are born than the environment can support, so there is a <b>struggle for survival</b>.	ImatPath Biology key idea
bio26-023	<small>Natural selection</small><br><b>Differential reproduction</b>	individuals with traits better suited to the environment <b>survive and reproduce more</b>, so their alleles become more common in the next generation.	ImatPath Biology key idea
bio26-024	<small>Natural selection</small><br><b>Antibiotic resistance</b>	in a population of bacteria exposed to an antibiotic, a few have a <b>mutation</b> that makes them resistant. Those survive and reproduce, so resistance becomes common. Using antibiotics too often or not finishing a course <b>selects</b> for resistance.	ImatPath Biology key idea
bio26-025	<small>Chance and evolution</small><br><b>Bottleneck effect</b>	a disaster sharply reduces the population. The survivors' alleles are a random sample, and diversity is lost (for example, the cheetah and the northern elephant seal).	ImatPath Biology key idea
bio26-026	<small>Chance and evolution</small><br><b>Founder effect</b>	a few individuals found a new population (for example, on an island). Their alleles may be rare in the original population.	ImatPath Biology key idea
bio26-027	<small>Answers & explanations</small><br><b>Genetic drift</b>	<b>bottleneck</b>, <b>founder effect</b>. <b>Gene flow</b> makes populations similar.	ImatPath Biology key idea
bio26-009	Key points: <b>Natural selection</b>	• <b>Variation, heritability, overproduction, differential reproduction</b>. Populations evolve, individuals do not. <b>Fitness</b> = reproductive success.<br>• <b>Directional</b> (one extreme), <b>stabilising</b> (average), <b>disruptive</b> (both extremes). <b>Sexual selection</b> for mating success.<br>• Examples: <b>peppered moth</b>, <b>antibiotic resistance</b>, Darwin's finches.	ImatPath Biology summary
bio26-010	Key points: <b>Artificial selection</b>	• Humans choose the breeders. Wild mustard → many vegetables. Reduces variation and raises inbreeding risk.	ImatPath Biology summary
bio26-011	Key points: <b>Chance</b>	• <b>Genetic drift</b> (strongest in small populations): <b>bottleneck</b>, <b>founder effect</b>. <b>Gene flow</b> makes populations similar.	ImatPath Biology summary
bio26-001	Beetles of genotype X leave on average 10 surviving offspring and genotype Y leave 20. What is the relative fitness of X, and the selection coefficient against it?<br><br><br>A) Fitness 2.0, selection coefficient 0<br>B) Fitness 0.5, selection coefficient 0.5<br>C) Fitness 0.5, selection coefficient 1.0<br>D) Fitness 10, selection coefficient 20<br>E) Fitness 1.0, selection coefficient 0.5	<b>B) Fitness 0.5, selection coefficient 0.5</b><br><br>Relative fitness is measured against the best genotype: <i>10/20 = 0.5</i>. The selection coefficient is <i>s = 1 - w = 0.5</i>.	ImatPath Biology practice
bio26-003	Two populations have an allele with a frequency of 0.5. One has 10 individuals and the other 1000. Which is expected to show larger random changes in allele frequency from generation to generation?<br><br><br>A) The population of 10, because genetic drift is stronger in small populations<br>B) The population of 1000, because it has more mutations<br>C) Both equally<br>D) Neither, because drift needs selection<br>E) The population of 1000, because it has more migration	<b>A) The population of 10, because genetic drift is stronger in small populations</b><br><br>Drift is random sampling of alleles. With few individuals, chance has a large effect on which alleles are passed on, and alleles can be lost or fixed quickly. Large populations average out.	ImatPath Biology practice
bio26-005	Male peacocks with larger tails are more attractive to females but are easier for predators to catch. How can the large tail persist?<br><br><br>A) It cannot persist, because natural selection always removes it<br>B) The tail helps the male to fly<br>C) The tail is inherited only by females<br>D) Predators prefer small tails<br>E) The mating advantage outweighs the survival cost, through sexual selection	<b>E) The mating advantage outweighs the survival cost, through sexual selection</b><br><br>Sexual selection favours traits that increase the number of offspring, even if they lower survival. What counts for fitness is reproductive success, not just survival.	ImatPath Biology practice
bio26-007	Gene flow between two populations of a plant by pollen transfer will tend to:<br><br><br>A) Make them more different<br>B) Eliminate mutation<br>C) Have no effect<br>D) Make their allele frequencies more similar<br>E) Increase genetic drift	<b>D) Make their allele frequencies more similar</b><br><br>Migrants bring alleles with them, so gene flow mixes the gene pools and reduces differences between populations, which counteracts divergence and speciation.	ImatPath Biology practice
