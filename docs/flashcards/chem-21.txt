chem21-001	<b>Functional group</b>: what is it?	A <b>functional group</b> is an <b>atom or group of atoms</b> in an organic molecule that is <b>responsible for its characteristic reactions</b>. Compounds with the same functional group behave in a similar way.	ImatPath Chemistry definition

chem21-033	<small>Alcohols</small><br><b>Primary</b>	the carbon bonded to OH has <b>one</b> other carbon (or none). Example: ethanol.	ImatPath Chemistry key idea
chem21-034	<small>Alcohols</small><br><b>Secondary</b>	the carbon has <b>two</b> other carbons. Example: propan-2-ol.	ImatPath Chemistry key idea
chem21-035	<small>Alcohols</small><br><b>Tertiary</b>	the carbon has <b>three</b> other carbons. Example: 2-methylpropan-2-ol.	ImatPath Chemistry key idea
chem21-036	<small>Alcohols</small><br><b>Combustion</b>	<i>C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>5</sub>OH + 3O<sub>2</sub> → 2CO<sub>2</sub> + 3H<sub>2</sub>O</i>. Ethanol is a biofuel.	ImatPath Chemistry key idea
chem21-002	Formula to know: <b>Carboxylic acids</b>	2CH<sub>3</sub>COOH + Na<sub>2</sub>CO<sub>3</sub> → 2CH<sub>3</sub>COONa + H<sub>2</sub>O + CO<sub>2</sub>	ImatPath Chemistry formula
chem21-003	Formula to know: <b>Making an ester</b>	CH<sub>3</sub>COOH + C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>5</sub>OH ⇌ CH<sub>3</sub>COOC<sub>2</sub>H<sub>5</sub> + H<sub>2</sub>O	ImatPath Chemistry formula
chem21-004	Formula to know: <b>Haloalkanes</b>	CH<sub>3</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>Br + OH<sup>-</sup> → CH<sub>3</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>OH + Br<sup>-</sup>	ImatPath Chemistry formula
chem21-013	Key points: <b>Alcohols</b>	• 1° → aldehyde → acid. 2° → ketone. 3° → no oxidation. Dichromate: orange → green.<br>• Dehydration gives an alkene (conc. H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub>).	ImatPath Chemistry summary
chem21-014	Key points: <b>Reactions to know</b>	• Acid + alcohol ⇌ ester + water (esterification, H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub>).<br>• Fat + NaOH → glycerol + soap (saponification).<br>• Two amino acids → dipeptide + H<sub>2</sub>O (condensation).<br>• Haloalkane + OH<sup>-</sup> → alcohol (nucleophilic substitution).	ImatPath Chemistry summary
chem21-015	Key points: <b>Biology links</b>	• Ethanol is oxidised in the liver to ethanal, then ethanoic acid. Methanol poisoning damages the optic nerve.<br>• Fats are triesters of glycerol. Proteins are chains of amino acids joined by peptide bonds.	ImatPath Chemistry summary
chem21-005	Butan-2-ol is heated under reflux with an excess of acidified potassium dichromate. What is the organic product?<br><br><br>A) Butanal<br>B) Butanone<br>C) Butanoic acid<br>D) Butane<br>E) No reaction	<b>B) Butanone</b><br><br>Butan-2-ol is a <b>secondary</b> alcohol. Oxidation gives a ketone (butanone), and there is no further oxidation because the carbonyl carbon has no H to lose. A primary alcohol would go on to an acid, and a tertiary alcohol would not react.	ImatPath Chemistry practice
chem21-007	Which are the products of the hydrolysis of CH<sub>3</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>COOCH<sub>2</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>CH<sub>3</sub>?<br><br><br>A) Propanoic acid and propan-1-ol<br>B) Ethanoic acid and butan-1-ol<br>C) Propanoic acid and propan-2-ol<br>D) Butanoic acid and ethanol<br>E) Propanal and propan-1-ol	<b>A) Propanoic acid and propan-1-ol</b><br><br>The part from the acid is CH<sub>3</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>CO– (3 carbons: propanoic acid). The part from the alcohol is –OCH<sub>2</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>CH<sub>3</sub> (propan-1-ol). The ester is called propyl propanoate.	ImatPath Chemistry practice
chem21-009	Which functional groups are present in CH<sub>3</sub>CH(OH)CH<sub>2</sub>COOCH<sub>3</sub>?<br><br><br>A) A primary alcohol and a carboxylic acid<br>B) A secondary alcohol and a carboxylic acid<br>C) A ketone and an ester<br>D) A tertiary alcohol and an ester<br>E) A secondary alcohol and an ester	<b>E) A secondary alcohol and an ester</b><br><br>CH(OH) is attached to two carbons, so it is a secondary alcohol. –COO–CH<sub>3</sub> is an ester (the acid part is joined to a methyl group). There is no free –COOH.	ImatPath Chemistry practice
chem21-011	Ethanol, phenol and ethanoic acid are tested with sodium hydrogencarbonate solution. Which gives bubbles of carbon dioxide, and what is the order of acidity?<br><br><br>A) Only phenol. Acidity: ethanoic acid < phenol < ethanol<br>D) Only ethanoic acid. Acidity: ethanol < phenol < ethanoic acid<br>E) All three. Acidity: ethanol < phenol < ethanoic acid	<b>D) Only ethanoic acid.
