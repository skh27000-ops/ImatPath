chem08-044	<b>oxidation number</b>: what is it?	The <b>oxidation number</b> of an atom is the <b>charge it would have if all its bonds were ionic</b>.	ImatPath Chemistry term
chem08-045	<b>oxide</b>: what is it?	An <b>oxide</b> is a compound of oxygen with another element, with oxygen at -2.	ImatPath Chemistry term
chem08-046	<b>hydroxide</b>: what is it?	A <b>hydroxide</b> contains the <b>OH<sup>-</sup></b> ion.	ImatPath Chemistry term
chem08-047	<b>acid</b>: what is it?	An <b>acid</b> produces <b>H<sup>+</sup> ions</b> in water (Brønsted and Lowry give a wider definition in lesson 16).	ImatPath Chemistry term
chem08-048	<b>salt</b>: what is it?	A <b>salt</b> is an <b>ionic compound made of a positive ion (usually a metal ion) and the negative ion of an acid</b>.	ImatPath Chemistry term

chem08-010	Key points: <b>Naming</b>	• Oxidation numbers: element 0, ion = its charge, O usually -2, H usually +1, compound adds to 0.<br>• Binary: <b>-ide</b>. Roman numerals for metals with several oxidation numbers: iron(III) oxide.<br>• More oxygen: <b>-ic acid / -ate</b>. Less oxygen: <b>-ous acid / -ite</b>.<br>• Chlorine: hypochlorite, chlorite, chlorate, perchlorate (1, 2, 3, 4 oxygens).	ImatPath Chemistry summary
chem08-011	Key points: <b>Reactions</b>	• Acid + base: salt + water. Acid + carbonate: salt + water + CO<sub>2</sub>. Acid + metal: salt + H<sub>2</sub>.<br>• Basic oxide + acid: salt + water. Acidic oxide + base: salt + water.	ImatPath Chemistry summary
chem08-012	Key points: <b>Solubility</b>	• Soluble: group 1 and ammonium salts, all nitrates, most chlorides and sulfates.<br>• Insoluble: most carbonates and phosphates, AgCl, BaSO<sub>4</sub>, PbSO<sub>4</sub>.	ImatPath Chemistry summary
chem08-002	What are the oxidation numbers of the two nitrogen atoms in ammonium nitrate, NH<sub>4</sub>NO<sub>3</sub>?<br><br><br>A) +1 and +1<br>B) -3 and +5<br>C) -3 and +3<br>D) +3 and +5<br>E) -5 and +3	<b>B) -3 and +5</b><br><br>The compound contains NH<sub>4</sub><sup>+</sup> and NO<sub>3</sub><sup>-</sup>. In NH<sub>4</sub><sup>+</sup>: <i>x + 4(+1) = +1</i>, so <i>x = -3</i>. In NO<sub>3</sub><sup>-</sup>: <i>x + 3(-2) = -1</i>, so <i>x = +5</i>.	ImatPath Chemistry practice
chem08-004	6.2 g of sodium oxide (<i>M = 62</i> g/mol) are added to water and the solution is made up to 500 cm<sup>3</sup>. What is the concentration of hydroxide ions?<br><br><br>A) 0.40 mol/L<br>B) 0.10 mol/L<br>C) 0.20 mol/L<br>D) 0.80 mol/L<br>E) 0.0040 mol/L	<b>A) 0.40 mol/L</b><br><br>Na<sub>2</sub>O + H<sub>2</sub>O → 2NaOH. <i>n(Na<sub>2</sub>O) = 6.2/62 = 0.10</i> mol, which gives 0.20 mol of NaOH, i.e. 0.20 mol of OH<sup>-</sup>. In 0.500 L: <i>0.20/0.500 = 0.40</i> mol/L.	ImatPath Chemistry practice
