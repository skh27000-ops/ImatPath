bio14-022	<b>Glycolysis</b>: what is it?	<b>Glycolysis</b> ("sugar splitting") breaks one 6-carbon <b>glucose</b> into two 3-carbon <b>pyruvate</b> molecules.	ImatPath Biology term

bio14-023	<small>Glycolysis</small><br><b>Investment phase</b>	the cell <b>spends 2 ATP</b> to add phosphate groups to glucose (so it becomes reactive and trapped in the cell).	ImatPath Biology key idea
bio14-024	<small>Glycolysis</small><br><b>Payoff phase</b>	each 3-carbon sugar is oxidised, giving <b>NADH</b>, and <b>4 ATP</b> are made by <b>substrate-level phosphorylation</b>.	ImatPath Biology key idea
bio14-025	<small>Answers & explanations</small><br><b>Link reaction</b>	pyruvate → acetyl CoA + CO<sub>2</sub> + NADH.	ImatPath Biology key idea
bio14-026	<small>Answers & explanations</small><br><b>Krebs cycle</b>	CO<sub>2</sub>, NADH, FADH<sub>2</sub>, ATP.	ImatPath Biology key idea
bio14-027	<small>Answers & explanations</small><br><b>Oxidative phosphorylation</b>	most ATP, <b>O<sub>2</sub> → H<sub>2</sub>O</b>.	ImatPath Biology key idea
bio14-001	Formula to know: <b>The equation</b>	C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>12</sub>O<sub>6</sub> + 6 O<sub>2</sub> → 6 CO<sub>2</sub> + 6 H<sub>2</sub>O + about 30–32 ATP	ImatPath Biology formula
bio14-002	Formula to know: <b>Splitting glucose</b>	Net per glucose: <b>2 ATP</b> + <b>2 NADH</b> + <b>2 pyruvate</b>	ImatPath Biology formula
bio14-011	Key points: <b>Stages</b>	• <b>Glycolysis</b> (cytoplasm): glucose → 2 pyruvate, net <b>2 ATP</b>, 2 NADH.<br>• <b>Link reaction</b> (matrix): pyruvate → acetyl CoA + CO<sub>2</sub> + NADH.<br>• <b>Krebs cycle</b> (matrix): CO<sub>2</sub>, NADH, FADH<sub>2</sub>, ATP.<br>• <b>Oxidative phosphorylation</b> (inner membrane): most ATP, <b>O<sub>2</sub> → H<sub>2</sub>O</b>.<br>• Total about <b>30–32 ATP</b>.	ImatPath Biology summary
bio14-012	Key points: <b>No oxygen</b>	• <b>Fermentation</b> regenerates NAD<sup>+</sup>; only <b>2 ATP</b>. Animals: lactate. Yeast: ethanol + CO<sub>2</sub>.	ImatPath Biology summary
bio14-003	One glucose is completely oxidised to CO<sub>2</sub> in a cell. In which stages is the CO<sub>2</sub> released, and how many CO<sub>2</sub> molecules are produced?<br><br><br>A) In glycolysis only; 2<br>B) In the link reaction and the Krebs cycle; 6<br>C) In the electron transport chain; 6<br>D) In the link reaction only; 2<br>E) In glycolysis and the link reaction; 4	<b>B) In the link reaction and the Krebs cycle; 6</b><br><br>Glycolysis makes no CO<sub>2</sub>. The link reaction releases 2 (one per pyruvate) and the Krebs cycle releases 4 (two per turn, two turns). Total: 6 CO<sub>2</sub>, one per carbon of glucose.	ImatPath Biology practice
bio14-005	A person at rest exhales 150 mL of CO<sub>2</sub> per minute and uses 200 mL of O<sub>2</sub> per minute. What is the respiratory quotient, and what does it suggest?<br><br><br>A) 0.75; mostly fat is being used<br>B) 1.33; mostly carbohydrate is being used<br>C) 0.75; mostly carbohydrate is being used<br>D) 1.0; a mixture of fat and protein<br>E) 1.33; mostly fat is being used	<b>A) 0.75; mostly fat is being used</b><br><br>RQ <i>= CO<sub>2</sub>/O<sub>2</sub> = 150/200 = 0.75</i>. Carbohydrates give RQ about 1.0 and fats about 0.7, so 0.75 suggests mostly fat, with some carbohydrate.	ImatPath Biology practice
bio14-007	A cell is deprived of oxygen. Which process can continue, and which stops?<br><br><br>A) The Krebs cycle continues; glycolysis stops<br>B) All stages continue at the same rate<br>C) The electron transport chain continues; glycolysis stops<br>D) Fermentation stops; glycolysis continues<br>E) Glycolysis continues (if NAD<sup>+</sup> is regenerated by fermentation); the Krebs cycle and the electron transport chain stop	<b>E) Glycolysis continues (if NAD<sup>+</sup> is regenerated by fermentation); the Krebs cycle and the electron transport chain stop</b><br><br>Without oxygen the chain cannot pass electrons, so NAD<sup>+</sup> is not regenerated and the Krebs cycle stops. Glycolysis can go on because fermentation regenerates NAD<sup>+</sup>, but it makes only 2 ATP per glucose.	ImatPath Biology practice
bio14-009	A fatty acid with 16 carbon atoms is oxidised completely. How many molecules of acetyl-CoA are produced by β-oxidation?<br><br><br>A) 16<br>B) 4<br>C) 2<br>D) 8<br>E) 32	<b>D) 8</b><br><br>β-oxidation removes two carbons at a time as acetyl-CoA, which then enters the Krebs cycle. <i>16/2 = 8</i>. This is why fats yield much more ATP than the same mass of carbohydrate.	ImatPath Biology practice
