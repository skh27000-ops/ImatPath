phys04-001	<b>Period and frequency</b>: what is it?	The <b>period</b> <i>T</i> is the time for one complete revolution. The <b>frequency</b> <i>f</i> is the number of revolutions per second. <i>f = 1/T</i>, measured in hertz (Hz).	ImatPath Physics definition
phys04-004	<b>SHM</b>: what is it?	In <b>simple harmonic motion</b>, <i>a = -ω<sup>2</sup> x</i>. The minus sign shows that the acceleration is always opposite to the displacement.	ImatPath Physics definition
phys04-019	<b>period</b>: what is it?	The <b>period</b> <i>T</i> is the time for one complete revolution.	ImatPath Physics term
phys04-020	<b>frequency</b>: what is it?	The <b>frequency</b> <i>f</i> is the number of revolutions per second.	ImatPath Physics term
phys04-021	<b>simple harmonic motion</b>: what is it?	In <b>simple harmonic motion</b>, <i>a = -ω<sup>2</sup> x</i>.	ImatPath Physics term
phys04-002	Formula to know: <b>Describing a circle</b>	<i>ω = (2π)/(T) = 2π f &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; v = ω r</i>	ImatPath Physics formula
phys04-003	Formula to know: <b>Centripetal acceleration and force</b>	<i>a = (v<sup>2</sup>)/(r) = ω<sup>2</sup> r &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; F = (mv<sup>2</sup>)/(r) = mω<sup>2</sup> r</i>	ImatPath Physics formula
phys04-005	Formula to know: <b>Equations and graphs</b>	<i>x = Acosω t &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; v = -Aωsinω t &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; a = -Aω<sup>2</sup>cosω t</i><br><i>v = ±ω√(A<sup>2</sup> - x<sup>2</sup>) &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; v<sub>max</sub> = Aω &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; a<sub>max</sub> = Aω<sup>2</sup></i>	ImatPath Physics formula
phys04-006	Formula to know: <b>Spring and pendulum</b>	Mass on a spring: <i>T = 2π√(m)/(k)</i><br>Simple pendulum: <i>T = 2π√(l)/(g)</i>	ImatPath Physics formula
phys04-007	Formula to know: <b>Kinetic and potential energy</b>	<i>E<sub>total</sub> = (1)/(2)kA<sup>2</sup> = (1)/(2)mω<sup>2</sup>A<sup>2</sup></i><br><i>E<sub>k</sub> = (1)/(2)mω<sup>2</sup>(A<sup>2</sup> - x<sup>2</sup>) &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; E<sub>p</sub> = (1)/(2)mω<sup>2</sup>x<sup>2</sup></i>	ImatPath Physics formula
phys04-016	Key points: <b>Circular motion</b>	• <i>T = 1/f</i>, <i>ω = 2π/T = 2π f</i>, <i>v = ω r</i>.<br>• <i>a = v<sup>2</sup>/r = ω<sup>2</sup> r</i> <b>towards the centre</b>. <i>F = mv<sup>2</sup>/r</i>. It is the <b>resultant</b> of real forces.<br>• Velocity is tangent to the circle. There is no outward "centrifugal force".	ImatPath Physics summary
phys04-017	Key points: <b>SHM</b>	• <i>a = -ω<sup>2</sup>x</i>. <i>x = Acosω t</i>. <i>v<sub>max</sub> = Aω</i>, <i>a<sub>max</sub> = Aω<sup>2</sup></i>.<br>• Spring <i>T = 2π√(m/k)</i>. Pendulum <i>T = 2π√(l/g)</i> (small angles).<br>• Speed is greatest at the centre. Acceleration is greatest at the ends.	ImatPath Physics summary
phys04-018	Key points: <b>Energy</b>	• <i>E<sub>total</sub> = (1)/(2)mω<sup>2</sup>A<sup>2</sup> ∝ A<sup>2</sup></i> and is constant without damping.<br>• Kinetic at the centre, potential at the ends.	ImatPath Physics summary
phys04-008	A car takes a flat curve of radius 80 m. The coefficient of static friction between the tyres and the road is 0.65 (<i>g = 9.8</i> m/s<sup>2</sup>). What is the maximum speed at which it can take the curve without skidding?<br><br><br>A) 510 m/s<br>B) 22.6 m/s<br>C) 28 m/s<br>D) 6.37 m/s<br>E) 0.282 m/s	<b>B) 22.6 m/s</b><br><br>Friction provides the centripetal force: <i>μ mg = mv<sup>2</sup>/r</i>, so <i>v = √(μ g r) = √(0.65 × 9.8 × 80) = 22.6</i> m/s. The mass cancels.	ImatPath Physics practice
phys04-010	The Moon orbits the Earth in a circle of radius <i>4.0 × 10<sup>8</sup></i> m with a period of 27.3 days. What is its centripetal acceleration?<br><br><br>A) 0.00284 m/s<sup>2</sup><br>B) 1070 m/s<sup>2</sup><br>C) 9.8 m/s<sup>2</sup><br>D) 0.0775 m/s<sup>2</sup><br>E) 1.04 × 10<sup>-4</sup> m/s<sup>2</sup>	<b>A) 0.00284 m/s<sup>2</sup></b><br><br>The period in seconds is <i>27.3 × 86 400 = 2.36 × 10<sup>6</sup></i> s. <i>v = 2π r/T = 1070</i> m/s and <i>a = v<sup>2</sup>/r = 0.00284</i> m/s<sup>2</sup>.	ImatPath Physics practice
phys04-012	A simple pendulum has a period of 2.50 s. Its length is shortened by 36%. What is the new period?<br><br><br>A) 1.60 s<br>B) 1.50 s<br>C) 1.25 s<br>D) 2.30 s<br>E) 2.00 s	<b>E) 2.00 s</b><br><br><i>T ∝ √(l)</i>. The new length is <i>0.64l</i>, so <i>T<sub>2</sub> = T√(0.64) = 0.80 × 2.50 = 2.00</i> s.	ImatPath Physics practice
phys04-014	A 0.50 kg mass hangs at rest from a spring and stretches it by 4.9 cm (<i>g = 9.8</i> m/s<sup>2</sup>). It is pulled down a little and released. What is the period of the oscillation?<br><br><br>A) 20.1 s<br>B) 2.25 s<br>C) 0.889 s<br>D) 0.444 s<br>E) 1.39 s	<b>D) 0.444 s</b><br><br><i>k = mg/x = (0.50)(9.8)/0.049 = 100</i> N/m. <i>T = 2π√(m/k) = 2π√(0.50/100) = 0.444</i> s.	ImatPath Physics practice
