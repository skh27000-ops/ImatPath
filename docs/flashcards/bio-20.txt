bio20-009	Key points: <b>Prokaryotes</b>	• One <b>circular</b> chromosome in the <b>nucleoid</b>, supercoiled, <b>no histones</b>, little non-coding DNA, genes in <b>operons</b>.<br>• <b>Plasmids</b>: extra circles, resistance genes. Gene transfer: <b>transformation, transduction, conjugation</b>.	ImatPath Biology summary
bio20-010	Key points: <b>Eukaryotes</b>	• DNA → <b>nucleosomes</b> (histone octamer) → 30-nm fibre → loops → <b>chromosome</b>.<br>• <b>Euchromatin</b> active, <b>heterochromatin</b> inactive (Barr body).<br>• Genes with <b>introns</b>; most DNA is non-coding. Human genome: ~3.1 billion bp, ~20,000 genes, 99.9% identical between people.	ImatPath Biology summary
bio20-001	A stretch of chromatin has 10 nucleosomes. Each nucleosome has 147 bp of DNA wrapped around the histone core, and each is separated by 53 bp of linker DNA. What is the total length of DNA (counting 10 repeats of core + linker)?<br><br><br>A) 1470 bp<br>B) 2000 bp<br>C) 530 bp<br>D) 2200 bp<br>E) 1000 bp	<b>B) 2000 bp</b><br><br>Each repeat is <i>147 + 53 = 200</i> bp. For 10 repeats: <i>10 × 200 = 2000</i> bp.	ImatPath Biology practice
bio20-003	A region of the nucleus stains darkly and the genes in it are rarely transcribed. What is it?<br><br><br>A) Heterochromatin<br>B) Euchromatin<br>C) The nucleolus<br>D) A plasmid<br>E) A telomere	<b>A) Heterochromatin</b><br><br>Heterochromatin is tightly packed, so RNA polymerase cannot reach the genes, and it is transcriptionally inactive. Euchromatin is loosely packed and active. The inactive X chromosome (Barr body) is an example of heterochromatin.	ImatPath Biology practice
bio20-005	A bacterium has one chromosome and two types of plasmid, each present in 10 copies. How many separate DNA molecules does it contain?<br><br><br>A) 3<br>B) 11<br>C) 20<br>D) 2<br>E) 21	<b>E) 21</b><br><br>One circular chromosome <i>+ 2 × 10</i> plasmid copies <i>= 21</i> DNA molecules. Plasmids replicate independently of the chromosome, so their copy number can be high.	ImatPath Biology practice
bio20-007	A fragment of a chromosome without a centromere is formed during cell division. What happens to it?<br><br><br>A) It is pulled to a pole by the kinetochore<br>B) It becomes a plasmid<br>C) It forms a telomere<br>D) It cannot attach to the spindle and is lost from the daughter cells<br>E) It is replicated twice	<b>D) It cannot attach to the spindle and is lost from the daughter cells</b><br><br>The centromere is where the kinetochore assembles and spindle microtubules attach. A fragment without a centromere is not pulled to either pole and is usually lost.	ImatPath Biology practice
