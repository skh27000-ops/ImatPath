bio12-002	In the reaction lactate + NAD<sup>+</sup> → pyruvate + NADH + H<sup>+</sup>, which statement is correct?<br><br><br>A) Lactate is reduced and NAD<sup>+</sup> is oxidised<br>B) Lactate is oxidised and NAD<sup>+</sup> is reduced<br>C) Both are oxidised<br>D) Both are reduced<br>E) Neither changes oxidation state	<b>B) Lactate is oxidised and NAD<sup>+</sup> is reduced</b><br><br>Lactate loses hydrogen (and electrons) to become pyruvate: oxidation. NAD<sup>+</sup> gains them to become NADH: reduction. Whenever one substance is oxidised, another is reduced.	ImatPath Biology practice
bio12-004	Why does FADH<sub>2</sub> produce less ATP than NADH?<br><br><br>A) It donates its electrons later in the chain, so fewer protons are pumped<br>B) It carries fewer electrons than NADH<br>C) It is made outside the mitochondrion<br>D) It donates electrons to oxygen directly<br>E) It cannot be oxidised	<b>A) It donates its electrons later in the chain, so fewer protons are pumped</b><br><br>FADH<sub>2</sub> gives electrons to a carrier further along the chain (complex II), bypassing the first proton pump. Fewer protons are pumped per electron pair, so less ATP is made (about 1.5 against 2.5).	ImatPath Biology practice
bio12-006	Per glucose, aerobic respiration forms 10 NADH and 2 FADH<sub>2</sub>. How many electrons do these carriers deliver to the electron transport chain? (Each carrier carries 2 electrons.)<br><br><br>A) 12<br>B) 10<br>C) 48<br>D) 36<br>E) 24	<b>E) 24</b><br><br>The number of carriers is <i>10 + 2 = 12</i>. Each carries 2 electrons: <i>12 × 2 = 24</i> electrons.	ImatPath Biology practice
bio12-008	Why does the electron transport chain move electrons through many carriers instead of passing them directly from NADH to oxygen?<br><br><br>A) It makes the electrons faster<br>B) It makes more oxygen<br>C) It makes electrons positive<br>D) The energy is released in small steps, which can be captured to pump protons instead of being lost as heat<br>E) It prevents ATP from forming	<b>D) The energy is released in small steps, which can be captured to pump protons instead of being lost as heat</b><br><br>A direct transfer would release all the energy at once, mostly as heat. Stepwise transfer lets each complex use a little energy to pump protons, building the gradient that drives ATP synthase.	ImatPath Biology practice
