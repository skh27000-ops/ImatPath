chem16-004	Formula to know: <b>Weak acids and the acid constant</b>	<i>K<sub>a</sub> = ([H<sup>+</sup>][A<sup>-</sup>])/([HA]) &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; pK<sub>a</sub> = -log K<sub>a</sub></i>	ImatPath Chemistry formula
chem16-005	Formula to know: <b>Weak acids and the acid constant</b>	<i>[H<sup>+</sup>] ≈ √(K<sub>a</sub> × c)</i>	ImatPath Chemistry formula
chem16-014	Key points: <b>Definitions</b>	• <b>Acid</b>: proton donor. <b>Base</b>: proton acceptor. Conjugate pair differs by one H<sup>+</sup>.<br>• <b>Strong</b>: fully ionised. <b>Weak</b>: partly ionised (equilibrium). Strength is not concentration.	ImatPath Chemistry summary
chem16-015	Key points: <b>pH</b>	• pH = <i>-log[H<sup>+</sup>]</i>. pOH = <i>-log[OH<sup>-</sup>]</i>. pH + pOH = 14. Each unit = ×10.<br>• Strong acid: <i>[H<sup>+</sup>] = c</i> (times 2 for H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub>). Strong base: <i>[OH<sup>-</sup>] = c</i>.<br>• Weak acid: <i>K<sub>a</sub> = [H<sup>+</sup>][A<sup>-</sup>]/[HA]</i>, <i>[H<sup>+</sup>] ≈ √(K<sub>a</sub> c)</i>.	ImatPath Chemistry summary
chem16-016	Key points: <b>Titration</b>	• <i>n = cV</i> for both, then use the equation ratio. Strong acid + strong base: equivalence at pH 7.<br>• Weak acid + strong base: above 7 (phenolphthalein). Weak base + strong acid: below 7 (methyl orange).<br>• Blood pH 7.35 to 7.45. Stomach pH 1.5 to 3.5. Acid rain below 5.6.	ImatPath Chemistry summary
chem16-006	What is the pH of 0.020 mol/L barium hydroxide, Ba(OH)<sub>2</sub>? Assume complete dissociation.<br><br><br>A) 12.3<br>B) 12.6<br>C) 1.4<br>D) 1.7<br>E) 12.0	<b>B) 12.6</b><br><br><i>[OH<sup>-</sup>] = 2 × 0.020 = 0.040</i> mol/L. <i>pOH = -log 0.040 = 1.40</i>. <i>pH = 14 - 1.40 = 12.6</i>.	ImatPath Chemistry practice
chem16-008	Equal volumes of hydrochloric acid of pH 1.00 and pH 3.00 are mixed. What is the pH of the mixture?<br><br><br>A) 1.30<br>B) 2.00<br>C) 1.00<br>D) 2.30<br>E) 0.70	<b>A) 1.30</b><br><br><i>[H<sup>+</sup>] = 0.100</i> and <i>0.0010</i> mol/L. Mixing equal volumes gives the mean: <i>(0.100 + 0.0010)/2 = 0.0505</i> mol/L. <i>pH = -log 0.0505 = 1.30</i>. Averaging the pH values (2.00) is wrong because pH is logarithmic.	ImatPath Chemistry practice
chem16-010	A 0.10 mol/L solution of a weak acid HA has a pH of 3.0. What is the acid dissociation constant <i>K<sub>a</sub></i>?<br><br><br>A) 1.0 × 10<sup>-3</sup><br>B) 1.0 × 10<sup>-6</sup><br>C) 1.0 × 10<sup>-4</sup><br>D) 1.0 × 10<sup>-7</sup><br>E) 1.0 × 10<sup>-5</sup>	<b>E) 1.0 × 10<sup>-5</sup></b><br><br><i>[H<sup>+</sup>] = [A<sup>-</sup>] = 10<sup>-3</sup></i> mol/L, and <i>[HA] = 0.10 - 0.001 ≈ 0.10</i>. <i>K<sub>a</sub> = (10<sup>-3</sup>)<sup>2</sup>/0.10 = 1.0 × 10<sup>-5</sup></i>. Only 1% of the acid is ionised.	ImatPath Chemistry practice
chem16-012	A solution has a pH of 9.5 at 25 °C. What is the concentration of hydroxide ions?<br><br><br>A) 3.2 × 10<sup>-10</sup> mol/L<br>B) 3.2 × 10<sup>-9</sup> mol/L<br>C) 4.5 mol/L<br>D) 3.2 × 10<sup>-5</sup> mol/L<br>E) 1.0 × 10<sup>-5</sup> mol/L	<b>D) 3.2 × 10<sup>-5</sup> mol/L</b><br><br><i>pOH = 14 - 9.5 = 4.5</i>. <i>[OH<sup>-</sup>] = 10<sup>-4.5</sup> = 3.2 × 10<sup>-5</sup></i> mol/L. (The <i>[H<sup>+</sup>]</i> is <i>3.2 × 10<sup>-10</sup></i>.)	ImatPath Chemistry practice
