bio21-018	<small>Regulation in bacteria</small><br><b>No lactose</b>	the <b>repressor</b> binds the <b>operator</b> and blocks RNA polymerase. The genes are <b>off</b>.	ImatPath Biology key idea
bio21-019	<small>Regulation in bacteria</small><br><b>Lactose present</b>	lactose (as <b>allolactose</b>) binds the <b>repressor</b> and changes its shape, so it <b>cannot bind</b> the operator. RNA polymerase transcribes the genes. The genes are <b>on</b>.	ImatPath Biology key idea

bio21-001	In which condition is the <i>lac</i> operon of <i>E. coli</i> expressed at a high level?<br><br><br>A) Lactose absent and glucose present<br>B) Lactose present and glucose absent<br>C) Lactose present and glucose present<br>D) Lactose absent and glucose absent<br>E) Only when tryptophan is present	<b>B) Lactose present and glucose absent</b><br><br>Lactose (as allolactose) removes the repressor from the operator, which allows transcription. When glucose is absent, cAMP and the CAP protein help RNA polymerase bind, giving a high level. If glucose is present, expression stays low even with lactose.	ImatPath Biology practice
bio21-003	The <i>trp</i> operon is a repressible operon. When tryptophan is abundant, what happens?<br><br><br>A) Tryptophan binds the repressor, which then blocks transcription<br>B) The repressor leaves the operator and the genes are switched on<br>C) Tryptophan is broken down by the operon enzymes<br>D) RNA polymerase is activated by cAMP<br>E) The operon is deleted	<b>A) Tryptophan binds the repressor, which then blocks transcription</b><br><br>Tryptophan acts as a co-repressor. It activates the repressor, which binds the operator and switches off the genes for making more tryptophan. This is negative feedback.	ImatPath Biology practice
bio21-005	A nucleus from a differentiated frog intestine cell is transferred into an egg without its nucleus, and develops into a tadpole. What does this show?<br><br><br>A) Differentiated cells lose genes they do not use<br>B) The egg cytoplasm is not needed<br>C) Mutations make cells identical<br>D) Tadpoles have no DNA<br>E) Differentiated cells keep all the genes, and differentiation depends on which genes are expressed	<b>E) Differentiated cells keep all the genes, and differentiation depends on which genes are expressed</b><br><br>The nucleus from a specialised cell still contained the complete genome, and the egg cytoplasm reprogrammed it to switch on the genes of an embryo. Cells differ by gene expression, not by gene content.	ImatPath Biology practice
bio21-007	A strain of <i>E. coli</i> has a mutation that destroys the lactose permease (<i>lacY</i>) but leaves the other genes normal. It is placed in a medium containing lactose as the only sugar. What happens?<br><br><br>A) It grows normally<br>B) It makes more permease<br>C) It grows by using the repressor<br>D) It grows very poorly, because lactose cannot enter in enough quantity<br>E) It dies because it makes too much β-galactosidase	<b>D) It grows very poorly, because lactose cannot enter in enough quantity</b><br><br>Permease brings lactose into the cell. Without it, almost no lactose enters, so the operon is not induced and the cell cannot use lactose as a source of energy.	ImatPath Biology practice
