chem17-001	<b>Hydrolysis</b>: what is it?	<b>Hydrolysis</b> of a salt is a reaction between <b>water</b> and an <b>ion of the salt</b>, which changes the <i>[H<sup>+</sup>]</i> or <i>[OH<sup>-</sup>]</i> of the solution.	ImatPath Chemistry definition
chem17-004	<b>Buffer</b>: what is it?	A <b>buffer</b> is a solution that <b>resists changes in pH</b> when small amounts of acid or base are added. It contains a <b>weak acid and its conjugate base</b> (or a weak base and its conjugate acid) <b>in similar, large amounts</b>.	ImatPath Chemistry definition

chem17-002	Formula to know: <b>Anions of weak acids make the solution alkaline</b>	CH<sub>3</sub>COO<sup>-</sup> + H<sub>2</sub>O ⇌ CH<sub>3</sub>COOH + OH<sup>-</sup>	ImatPath Chemistry formula
chem17-003	Formula to know: <b>Cations of weak bases make the solution acidic</b>	NH<sub>4</sub><sup>+</sup> + H<sub>2</sub>O ⇌ NH<sub>3</sub> + H<sub>3</sub>O<sup>+</sup>	ImatPath Chemistry formula
chem17-005	Formula to know: <b>The Henderson-Hasselbalch equation</b>	<i>pH = pK<sub>a</sub> + log<sub>10</sub>([A<sup>-</sup>])/([HA])</i>	ImatPath Chemistry formula
chem17-014	Key points: <b>Hydrolysis of salts</b>	• The ion from the <b>weak</b> parent reacts with water. CH<sub>3</sub>COO<sup>-</sup> + H<sub>2</sub>O ⇌ CH<sub>3</sub>COOH + OH<sup>-</sup>. NH<sub>4</sub><sup>+</sup> + H<sub>2</sub>O ⇌ NH<sub>3</sub> + H<sub>3</sub>O<sup>+</sup>.<br>• Al<sup>3+</sup> and Fe<sup>3+</sup> give acidic solutions.	ImatPath Chemistry summary
chem17-015	Key points: <b>Buffers</b>	• A weak acid and its conjugate base (or weak base and its conjugate acid), both in large amounts.<br>• Added H<sup>+</sup> reacts with the <b>base</b>. Added OH<sup>-</sup> reacts with the <b>acid</b>.<br>• <i>pH = pK<sub>a</sub> + log([A<sup>-</sup>]/[HA])</i>. Equal amounts: pH = <i>pK<sub>a</sub></i>. Works within ±1 of <i>pK<sub>a</sub></i>.<br>• Capacity is limited and grows with concentration.	ImatPath Chemistry summary
chem17-016	Key points: <b>In the body</b>	• Blood: H<sub>2</sub>CO<sub>3</sub> / HCO<sub>3</sub><sup>-</sup>, pH = 6.1 + log 20 = 7.4. Lungs control CO<sub>2</sub>, kidneys control HCO<sub>3</sub><sup>-</sup>.<br>• Phosphate and proteins buffer cells.	ImatPath Chemistry summary
chem17-006	A buffer contains 0.20 mol/L NH<sub>3</sub> and 0.10 mol/L NH<sub>4</sub>Cl. The p<i>K<sub>a</sub></i> of NH<sub>4</sub><sup>+</sup> is 9.25. What is the pH?<br><br><br>A) 9.25<br>B) 9.55<br>C) 8.95<br>D) 9.85<br>E) 4.45	<b>B) 9.55</b><br><br>Use <i>pH = pK<sub>a</sub> + log([base]/[acid])</i>. The base is NH<sub>3</sub> and the acid is NH<sub>4</sub><sup>+</sup>: <i>9.25 + log(0.20/0.10) = 9.25 + 0.30 = 9.55</i>.	ImatPath Chemistry practice
chem17-008	A buffer with a pH of 5.06 is to be made from ethanoic acid (p<i>K<sub>a</sub></i> = 4.76) and sodium ethanoate. What must the ratio [ethanoate]/[ethanoic acid] be?<br><br><br>A) 2.0<br>B) 0.50<br>C) 1.0<br>D) 1.3<br>E) 10	<b>A) 2.0</b><br><br><i>5.06 = 4.76 + log(ratio)</i>, so <i>log(ratio) = 0.30</i> and the ratio <i>= 10<sup>0.30</sup> = 2.0</i>.	ImatPath Chemistry practice
chem17-010	How many of these five 0.1 mol/L salt solutions have a pH <b>above</b> 7? NaNO<sub>3</sub>, KF, NH<sub>4</sub>Br, Na<sub>2</sub>CO<sub>3</sub>, CH<sub>3</sub>COONa<br><br><br>A) 1<br>B) 2<br>C) 4<br>D) 5<br>E) 3	<b>E) 3</b><br><br>F<sup>-</sup>, CO<sub>3</sub><sup>2-</sup> and CH<sub>3</sub>COO<sup>-</sup> are conjugate bases of weak acids, so they hydrolyse to give OH<sup>-</sup>. NaNO<sub>3</sub> comes from a strong acid and strong base (neutral). NH<sub>4</sub>Br is acidic because NH<sub>4</sub><sup>+</sup> is a weak acid.	ImatPath Chemistry practice
chem17-012	Which mixture is the best buffer for a solution at pH 9.2?<br><br><br>A) 0.10 mol/L CH<sub>3</sub>COOH with 0.10 mol/L CH<sub>3</sub>COONa (p<i>K<sub>a</sub></i> = 4.76)<br>B) 0.10 mol/L HCl with 0.10 mol/L NaCl<br>C) 0.10 mol/L HCOOH with 0.10 mol/L HCOONa (p<i>K<sub>a</sub></i> = 3.75)<br>D) 0.10 mol/L NH<sub>3</sub> with 0.10 mol/L NH<sub>4</sub>Cl (p<i>K<sub>a</sub></i> of NH<sub>4</sub><sup>+</sup> = 9.25)<br>E) 0.10 mol/L NaOH with 0.10 mol/L NaCl	<b>D) 0.10 mol/L NH<sub>3</sub> with 0.10 mol/L NH<sub>4</sub>Cl (p<i>K<sub>a</sub></i> of NH<sub>4</sub><sup>+</sup> = 9.25)</b><br><br>A buffer works best when the pH is within 1 unit of the p<i>K<sub>a</sub></i> and the two forms are present in similar amounts. Only the NH<sub>3</sub>/NH<sub>4</sub><sup>+</sup> pair has a p<i>K<sub>a</sub></i> close to 9.2. HCl and NaOH are strong, so they are not buffers.	ImatPath Chemistry practice
