phys12-003	<b>Specific latent heat</b>: what is it?	The <b>specific latent heat</b> <i>L</i> is the energy needed to change the state of <b>1 kg</b> of a substance <b>without changing its temperature</b>: <i>Q = mL</i>. The unit is J/kg.	ImatPath Physics definition

phys12-012	Key points: <b>Heat transfer</b>	• <b>Conduction</b> (solids): <i>Q/t = kAΔ T/L</i>. <b>Convection</b> (fluids): warm, less dense fluid rises. <b>Radiation</b>: <i>P = eσ AT<sup>4</sup></i>, T in kelvin, no medium needed.	ImatPath Physics summary
phys12-013	Key points: <b>Changes of state</b>	• <i>Q = mL</i> at a constant temperature. Water: <i>L<sub>f</sub> = 3.34 × 10<sup>5</sup></i>, <i>L<sub>v</sub> = 2.26 × 10<sup>6</sup></i> J/kg.<br>• Heating curve: slopes use <i>mcΔ T</i>, flat parts use <i>mL</i>.	ImatPath Physics summary
phys12-014	Key points: <b>Evaporation and humidity</b>	• Evaporation: any temperature, surface only, cools the liquid. Boiling: one temperature, whole liquid.<br>• Boiling point falls with lower pressure. Relative humidity = vapour pressure / saturated vapour pressure.	ImatPath Physics summary
phys12-004	0.10 kg of ice at <i>-10</i> °C is added to 0.50 kg of water at 30 °C (<i>c<sub>ice</sub> = 2100</i>, <i>L<sub>f</sub> = 3.34 × 10<sup>5</sup></i> J/kg). What is the final temperature?<br><br><br>A) 28.3 °C<br>B) 10.9 °C<br>C) 0.0 °C<br>D) 23.3 °C<br>E) 21.8 °C	<b>B) 10.9 °C</b><br><br>Heat to warm and melt the ice: <i>0.10(2100)(10) + 0.10(3.34 × 10<sup>5</sup>) = 35 500</i> J. The water can give up <i>0.50(4200)(30) = 63 000</i> J cooling to 0 °C, so all the ice melts. The remaining <i>27 500</i> J warms 0.60 kg: <i>T = 27 500/(0.60 × 4200) = 10.9</i> °C. phys12-006	A wall of area 10 m<sup>2</sup> has a brick layer (thickness 0.20 m, <i>k = 0.60</i> W m<sup>-1</sup> K<sup>-1</sup>) and an insulation layer (thickness 0.10 m, <i>k = 0.04</i> W m<sup>-1</sup> K<sup>-1</sup>). The temperature difference across the wall is 25 K. What is the rate of heat flow?<br><br><br>A) 88.2 W<br>B) 750 W<br>C) 100 W<br>D) 425 W<br>E) 176 W	<b>A) 88.2 W</b><br><br>The layers are in series, so the thermal resistances (per unit area) add: <i>L/k = 0.20/0.60 + 0.10/0.04 = 0.333 + 2.50 = 2.83</i> m<sup>2</sup>K/W. <i>P = AΔ T/∑(L/k) = 10 × 25/2.83 = 88.2</i> W.
