chem09-001	<b>The mole</b>: what is it?	One <b>mole</b> is the amount of substance that contains the same number of particles as there are atoms in <b>12 g of carbon-12</b>. This number is the <b>Avogadro constant</b>:<br><i>N<sub>A</sub> = 6.02 × 10<sup>23</sup> mol<sup>-1</sup></i>	ImatPath Chemistry definition
chem09-019	<b>mole</b>: what is it?	One <b>mole</b> is the amount of substance that contains the same number of particles as there are atoms in <b>12 g of carbon-12</b>.	ImatPath Chemistry term
chem09-020	<b>empirical formula</b>: what is it?	The <b>empirical formula</b> is the <b>simplest whole-number ratio</b> of the atoms of each element in a compound.	ImatPath Chemistry term
chem09-016	<small>Masses of atoms and molecules</small><br><b>Relative atomic mass, <i>A<sub>r</sub></i></b><br><i>Meaning · Example?</i>	<b>Meaning:</b> Average mass of an atom of an element compared with <i>(1)/(12)</i> of carbon-12<br><b>Example:</b> <i>A<sub>r</sub>(O) = 16</i>	ImatPath Chemistry recall
chem09-017	<small>Masses of atoms and molecules</small><br><b>Relative molecular mass, <i>M<sub>r</sub></i></b><br><i>Meaning · Example?</i>	<b>Meaning:</b> Sum of the <i>A<sub>r</sub></i> of all atoms in a molecule<br><b>Example:</b> <i>M<sub>r</sub>(H<sub>2</sub>O) = 2 × 1 + 16 = 18</i>	ImatPath Chemistry recall
chem09-018	<small>Masses of atoms and molecules</small><br><b>Relative formula mass, <i>M<sub>r</sub></i></b><br><i>Meaning · Example?</i>	<b>Meaning:</b> The same, for an ionic compound's formula<br><b>Example:</b> <i>M<sub>r</sub>(NaCl) = 23 + 35.5 = 58.5</i>	ImatPath Chemistry recall
chem09-002	Formula to know: <b>The three conversions</b>	<i>n = (m)/(M) &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; n = (N)/(N<sub>A</sub>) &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; n = (V)/(V<sub>m</sub>)</i>	ImatPath Chemistry formula
chem09-003	Formula to know: <b>Percentage composition by mass</b>	<i>%  element = (mass of the element in one mole)/(M<sub>r</sub>  of the compound) × 100</i>	ImatPath Chemistry formula
chem09-004	Formula to know: <b>From empirical to molecular formula</b>	<i>multiple = (M<sub>r</sub>(molecular))/(M<sub>r</sub>(empirical))</i>	ImatPath Chemistry formula
chem09-013	Key points: <b>Masses</b>	• <i>A<sub>r</sub></i>, <i>M<sub>r</sub></i>: relative to <i>(1)/(12)</i> of carbon-12, <b>no units</b>.<br>• Molar mass <i>M</i>: same number, units <b>g/mol</b>.<br>• Count every atom inside brackets: Ca(OH)<sub>2</sub> has 2 O and 2 H.	ImatPath Chemistry summary
chem09-014	Key points: <b>The mole</b>	• 1 mol = <i>6.02 × 10<sup>23</sup></i> particles (<i>N<sub>A</sub></i>).<br>• <i>n = m/M &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; n = N/N<sub>A</sub> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; n = V/V<sub>m</sub></i> (22.4 L/mol at STP).<br>• Always find the <b>moles first</b>, then convert.	ImatPath Chemistry summary
chem09-015	Key points: <b>Composition</b>	• % element = (mass of the element in one mole / <i>M<sub>r</sub></i>) × 100.<br>• <b>Empirical formula</b>: divide masses by <i>A<sub>r</sub></i>, then by the smallest, make whole numbers.<br>• <b>Molecular formula</b> = empirical × (<i>M<sub>r</sub></i> molecular / <i>M<sub>r</sub></i> empirical).	ImatPath Chemistry summary
chem09-005	A hydrocarbon contains 85.7% carbon and 14.3% hydrogen by mass, and its molar mass is 56 g/mol. What is its molecular formula?<br><br><br>A) CH<sub>2</sub><br>B) C<sub>4</sub>H<sub>8</sub><br>C) C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>4</sub><br>D) C<sub>4</sub>H<sub>10</sub><br>E) C<sub>5</sub>H<sub>8</sub>	<b>B) C<sub>4</sub>H<sub>8</sub></b><br><br>In 100 g: C <i>= 85.7/12 = 7.14</i> mol, H <i>= 14.3/1 = 14.3</i> mol. Ratio <i>= 1 : 2</i>, so the empirical formula is CH<sub>2</sub> (<i>M = 14</i>). <i>56/14 = 4</i>, so the molecular formula is C<sub>4</sub>H<sub>8</sub>.	ImatPath Chemistry practice
chem09-007	How many hydrogen atoms are in 5.0 g of ammonia, NH<sub>3</sub>? (<i>M = 17</i> g/mol)<br><br><br>A) 5.31 × 10<sup>23</sup><br>B) 1.77 × 10<sup>23</sup><br>C) 7.08 × 10<sup>23</sup><br>D) 9.03 × 10<sup>24</sup><br>E) 0.882	<b>A) 5.31 × 10<sup>23</sup></b><br><br><i>n = 5.0/17 = 0.294</i> mol of NH<sub>3</sub>. Each molecule has 3 H atoms, so <i>n(H) = 0.882</i> mol. Number <i>= 0.882 × 6.02 × 10<sup>23</sup> = 5.3 × 10<sup>23</sup></i>.	ImatPath Chemistry practice
chem09-009	What mass of ammonium nitrate (NH<sub>4</sub>NO<sub>3</sub>) contains the same mass of nitrogen as 1.00 kg of urea, CO(NH<sub>2</sub>)<sub>2</sub>? (<i>A<sub>r</sub></i>: H 1, C 12, N 14, O 16)<br><br><br>A) 0.75 kg<br>B) 1.00 kg<br>C) 2.14 kg<br>D) 0.47 kg<br>E) 1.33 kg	<b>E) 1.33 kg</b><br><br>Urea (<i>M = 60</i>) is <i>28/60 = 46.7%</i> N, so 1.00 kg contains 0.467 kg of N. Ammonium nitrate (<i>M = 80</i>) is <i>28/80 = 35%</i> N. Mass needed <i>= 0.467/0.35 = 1.33</i> kg.	ImatPath Chemistry practice
chem09-011	How many electrons are in 1.8 g of water? (<i>M = 18</i> g/mol, each H<sub>2</sub>O molecule has 10 electrons)<br><br><br>A) 6.02 × 10<sup>22</sup><br>B) 6.02 × 10<sup>24</sup><br>C) 1.8 × 10<sup>23</sup><br>D) 6.02 × 10<sup>23</sup><br>E) 3.01 × 10<sup>23</sup>	<b>D) 6.02 × 10<sup>23</sup></b><br><br><i>n = 1.8/18 = 0.10</i> mol of molecules, which is <i>6.02 × 10<sup>22</sup></i> molecules. Each has <i>2(1) + 8 = 10</i> electrons, so <i>6.02 × 10<sup>23</sup></i> electrons.	ImatPath Chemistry practice
