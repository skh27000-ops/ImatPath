bio18-009	Key points: <b>Structure</b>	• Nucleotide: deoxyribose + phosphate + base. <b>A–T (2 H-bonds), G–C (3)</b>. <b>Antiparallel</b> 5′→3′ / 3′→5′. Chargaff: A = T, G = C.<br>• Griffith → Avery → Hershey–Chase: DNA is the genetic material.	ImatPath Biology summary
bio18-010	Key points: <b>Replication</b>	• <b>Semi-conservative</b> (Meselson–Stahl). Origins, <b>helicase</b>, <b>primase</b>, <b>DNA polymerase</b> (adds to 3′), <b>ligase</b>.<br>• <b>Leading</b> (continuous), <b>lagging</b> (Okazaki fragments).<br>• Proofreading and repair keep errors very low. <b>Telomeres</b> protect chromosome ends (telomerase). bio18-001	A DNA sample contains 22% cytosine. What percentage of the bases is adenine?<br><br><br>A) 22%<br>B) 28%<br>C) 44%<br>D) 56%<br>E) 78%	<b>B) 28%</b><br><br>By Chargaff's rules <i>G = C = 22%</i>, so <i>G + C = 44%</i>. The rest is <i>56%</i> shared equally between <i>A</i> and <i>T</i>: <i>A = 28%</i>.	ImatPath Biology practice
bio18-003	Bacteria grown for many generations in heavy nitrogen (<sup>15</sup>N) are moved to light nitrogen (<sup>14</sup>N) and allowed to replicate three times. What fraction of the DNA molecules still contains any <sup>15</sup>N?<br><br><br>A) 1/4<br>B) 1/8<br>C) 1/2<br>D) 1/3<br>E) All	<b>A) 1/4</b><br><br>Replication is semi-conservative. Each of the two original <sup>15</sup>N strands ends up in one molecule only. After three replications there are 8 molecules and only 2 contain an original strand: <i>2/8 = 1/4</i> (hybrids with one <sup>15</sup>N strand and one <sup>14</sup>N strand).	ImatPath Biology practice
bio18-005	A cell lacks DNA ligase. What is the effect on DNA replication?<br><br><br>A) The leading strand cannot start<br>B) Primers cannot be made<br>C) The two strands cannot separate<br>D) Nucleotides cannot be added at all<br>E) Short Okazaki fragments accumulate on the lagging strand and are not joined	<b>E) Short Okazaki fragments accumulate on the lagging strand and are not joined</b><br><br>DNA ligase joins the Okazaki fragments of the lagging strand into a continuous strand. Helicase, primase and DNA polymerase work normally, but the lagging strand stays in pieces.	ImatPath Biology practice
bio18-007	Why do chromosomes become shorter at their ends after each round of replication in cells without telomerase?<br><br><br>A) The ends are digested by lysosomes<br>B) The leading strand is made in the wrong direction<br>C) Telomeres are made of RNA<br>D) DNA polymerase cannot fully replace the RNA primer at the end of the lagging strand<br>E) Ligase removes the ends	<b>D) DNA polymerase cannot fully replace the RNA primer at the end of the lagging strand</b><br><br>DNA polymerase adds nucleotides only to a 3' end, so the last primer at the 5' end of the new strand cannot be replaced. Each replication leaves a short gap. Telomerase adds repeats to the template to avoid the shortening.	ImatPath Biology practice
