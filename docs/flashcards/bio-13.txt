bio13-020	<small>Light-dependent reactions</small><br><b>Photolysis</b>	light excites PSII, and electrons are passed to the electron transport chain. They are replaced by splitting <b>water</b>: 2 H<sub>2</sub>O → 4 H<sup>+</sup> + 4 e<sup>−</sup> + O<sub>2</sub>. <b>This is the source of the oxygen</b>.	ImatPath Biology key idea
bio13-021	<small>Light-dependent reactions</small><br><b>Electron transport chain</b>	electrons move down the chain. The energy released pumps <b>H<sup>+</sup> into the thylakoid space</b>, making a proton gradient.	ImatPath Biology key idea
bio13-022	<small>Light-dependent reactions</small><br><b>Photophosphorylation</b>	protons flow back through <b>ATP synthase</b>, making <b>ATP</b> (chemiosmosis).	ImatPath Biology key idea
bio13-023	<small>The Calvin cycle</small><br><b>Carbon fixation</b>	the enzyme <b>rubisco</b> attaches CO<sub>2</sub> to a 5-carbon sugar, <b>RuBP</b>. The 6-carbon product splits at once into two molecules of <b>3-phosphoglycerate (3-PGA)</b>.	ImatPath Biology key idea
bio13-024	<small>The Calvin cycle</small><br><b>Reduction</b>	ATP and NADPH convert 3-PGA into <b>glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate (G3P)</b>, a 3-carbon sugar.	ImatPath Biology key idea
bio13-025	<small>The Calvin cycle</small><br><b>Regeneration</b>	most of the G3P is rearranged, using more ATP, to remake <b>RuBP</b>, so the cycle can continue.	ImatPath Biology key idea

CO<sub>2</sub> is <b>reduced</b>, H<sub>2</sub>O is <b>oxidised</b>.<br>• Light reactions in <b>thylakoids</b>, Calvin cycle in the <b>stroma</b>.	ImatPath Biology summary
bio13-011	Key points: <b>Light reactions</b>	• Light excites PSII → ETC pumps H<sup>+</sup> → <b>ATP synthase</b> makes ATP. PSI → <b>NADPH</b>. <b>Water splitting gives O<sub>2</sub></b>.	ImatPath Biology summary
bio13-012	Key points: <b>Calvin cycle</b>	• <b>Fix</b> (rubisco + RuBP) → <b>reduce</b> (ATP, NADPH → G3P) → <b>regenerate</b> RuBP. 3 turns = 1 net G3P.	ImatPath Biology summary
bio13-013	Key points: <b>Rate and adaptation</b>	• Limiting factors: light, CO<sub>2</sub>, temperature. C<sub>4</sub> and CAM plants reduce photorespiration and water loss.	ImatPath Biology summary
bio13-002	Plants are given water containing the heavy isotope <sup>18</sup>O and ordinary CO<sub>2</sub>. In which product will <sup>18</sup>O be found?<br><br><br>A) Glucose only<br>B) Oxygen gas (O<sub>2</sub>)<br>C) Carbon dioxide<br>D) ATP<br>E) NADPH	<b>B) Oxygen gas (O<sub>2</sub>)</b><br><br>The oxygen released in photosynthesis comes from the <b>splitting of water</b> (photolysis) in photosystem II. Glucose gets its oxygen atoms from CO<sub>2</sub>.	ImatPath Biology practice
bio13-004	How many ATP and NADPH are needed in the Calvin cycle to make one molecule of glucose (6 CO<sub>2</sub> fixed)?<br><br><br>A) 18 ATP and 12 NADPH<br>B) 6 ATP and 6 NADPH<br>C) 12 ATP and 18 NADPH<br>D) 9 ATP and 6 NADPH<br>E) 36 ATP and 24 NADPH	<b>A) 18 ATP and 12 NADPH</b><br><br>Fixing one CO<sub>2</sub> uses 3 ATP and 2 NADPH. For 6 CO<sub>2</sub>: <i>6 × 3 = 18</i> ATP and <i>6 × 2 = 12</i> NADPH.	ImatPath Biology practice
bio13-006	A plant is grown under green light only. Why is photosynthesis very slow?<br><br><br>A) Chlorophyll absorbs green light best<br>B) Green light has too much energy<br>C) Plants cannot see green<br>D) Green light stops the stomata from closing<br>E) Chlorophyll reflects most green light and absorbs mainly red and blue	<b>E) Chlorophyll reflects most green light and absorbs mainly red and blue</b><br><br>The action spectrum of photosynthesis matches the absorption of chlorophyll a and b: peaks in the blue and red regions. Green light is mostly reflected (which is why leaves look green), so little energy is captured.	ImatPath Biology practice
bio13-008	What does cyclic electron flow around photosystem I produce?<br><br><br>A) ATP, NADPH and O<sub>2</sub><br>B) NADPH only<br>C) O<sub>2</sub> and NADPH<br>D) ATP only, with no NADPH and no O<sub>2</sub><br>E) Glucose	<b>D) ATP only, with no NADPH and no O<sub>2</sub></b><br><br>In cyclic flow, electrons return to photosystem I and pass through the chain again. Protons are pumped, so ATP is made, but photosystem II is not used (so no water is split and no O<sub>2</sub> forms), and no NADP<sup>+</sup> is reduced.	ImatPath Biology practice
