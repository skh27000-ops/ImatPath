phys18-004	<b>Magnetic flux</b>: what is it?	The <b>magnetic flux</b> through a surface of area <i>A</i> is <i>Φ = BAcosθ</i>, where <i>θ</i> is the angle between the field and the <b>normal</b> to the surface. The unit is the <b>weber (Wb)</b>: 1 Wb = 1 T m<sup>2</sup>.	ImatPath Physics definition
phys18-005	<b>Faraday's law</b>: what is it?	The magnitude of the <b>induced emf</b> in a circuit equals the <b>rate of change of magnetic flux linkage</b>: <i>ε = N(ΔΦ)/(Δ t)</i>.	ImatPath Physics definition
phys18-006	<b>Lenz's law</b>: what is it?	The direction of the induced current is such that it <b>opposes the change that produced it</b>.	ImatPath Physics definition
phys18-018	<small>Magnetic fields</small><br><b>Straight wire</b>	circular field lines round the wire. Use the <b>right-hand grip rule</b>: thumb along the current, curled fingers give the field direction. Field strength at distance <i>r</i>: <i>B = (μ<sub>0</sub>I)/(2π r)</i>.	ImatPath Physics key idea
phys18-019	<small>Magnetic fields</small><br><b>Solenoid</b>	the field inside is <b>uniform</b> and the outside field looks like a bar magnet. <i>B = μ<sub>0</sub>nI</i>, where <i>n</i> is the number of turns per metre.	ImatPath Physics key idea
phys18-020	<small>Electromagnetic induction</small><br><b>AC generator</b>	a coil <b>rotates</b> in a magnetic field. The flux changes continuously and a sinusoidal emf is produced: <i>ε = NBAωsinω t</i>, with a peak of <i>ε<sub>0</sub> = NBAω</i>. A faster rotation or more turns gives a larger emf. This is how power stations and bicycle dynamos produce electricity.	ImatPath Physics key idea
phys18-021	<small>Electromagnetic induction</small><br><b>Electric motor</b>	the reverse. A current-carrying coil in a field feels a <b>torque</b> (lesson 7) and rotates. A <b>commutator</b> reverses the current every half turn so that the rotation continues.	ImatPath Physics key idea
phys18-001	Formula to know: <b>Force on a moving charge</b>	<i>F = qvBsinθ</i>	ImatPath Physics formula
phys18-002	Formula to know: <b>Force on a moving charge</b>	<i>qvB = (mv<sup>2</sup>)/(r) ⇒ r = (mv)/(qB)</i>	ImatPath Physics formula
phys18-003	Formula to know: <b>Force on a current-carrying wire</b>	<i>F = BILsinθ</i>	ImatPath Physics formula
phys18-015	Key points: <b>Fields</b>	• <i>B</i> in tesla. Wire: <i>B = μ<sub>0</sub>I/2π r</i>. Solenoid: <i>B = μ<sub>0</sub>nI</i>. <i>μ<sub>0</sub> = 4π × 10<sup>-7</sup></i> T m/A.	ImatPath Physics summary
phys18-016	Key points: <b>Forces</b>	• Charge: <i>F = qvBsinθ</i>, perpendicular to <i>v</i> and <i>B</i>, no work. Circle: <i>r = mv/(qB)</i>.<br>• Wire: <i>F = BILsinθ</i> (Fleming's left hand). Parallel currents attract.	ImatPath Physics summary
phys18-017	Key points: <b>Induction</b>	• <i>Φ = BAcosθ</i>. <i>ε = -NΔΦ/Δ t</i> (Faraday + Lenz). Moving rod: <i>ε = BLv</i>.<br>• Generator: <i>ε<sub>0</sub> = NBAω</i>. Motor: current in a field gives a torque.	ImatPath Physics summary
phys18-007	A proton moves in a circle of radius 0.20 m in a magnetic field of 0.50 T. What is its kinetic energy in MeV? (<i>m<sub>p</sub> = 1.67 × 10<sup>-27</sup></i> kg, <i>1 MeV = 1.6 × 10<sup>-13</sup></i> J)<br><br><br>A) 0.96 MeV<br>B) 0.48 MeV<br>C) 0.24 MeV<br>D) 1.9 MeV<br>E) 4.8 MeV	<b>B) 0.48 MeV</b><br><br><i>r = mv/(qB)</i>, so <i>v = qBr/m = 1.6 × 10<sup>-19</sup>(0.50)(0.20)/1.67 × 10<sup>-27</sup> = 9.58 × 10<sup>6</sup></i> m/s. <i>E<sub>k</sub> = (1)/(2)mv<sup>2</sup> = 7.7 × 10<sup>-14</sup></i> J <i>= 0.48</i> MeV. phys18-009	A straight wire 0.30 m long carries 4.0 A in a uniform field of 0.50 T. The wire makes an angle of 30° with the field. What is the force on it?<br><br><br>A) 0.30 N<br>B) 0.60 N<br>C) 0.52 N<br>D) 0.15 N<br>E) 1.2 N	<b>A) 0.30 N</b><br><br><i>F = BILsinθ = 0.50 × 4.0 × 0.30 × sin 30<sup>°</sup> = 0.30</i> N. phys18-011	A generator coil of 100 turns and area 0.020 m<sup>2</sup> rotates at 50 Hz in a field of 0.30 T. What is the rms emf?<br><br><br>A) 188 V<br>B) 21.2 V<br>C) 267 V<br>D) 94.2 V<br>E) 133 V	<b>E) 133 V</b><br><br><i>ω = 2π × 50 = 314</i> rad/s. Peak emf <i>= NBAω = 100 × 0.30 × 0.020 × 314 = 188</i> V. <i>ε<sub>rms</sub> = 188/√(2) = 133</i> V.	ImatPath Physics practice
phys18-013	A solenoid has 800 turns over a length of 0.40 m and carries a current of 2.0 A. What is the magnetic flux density inside it? (<i>μ<sub>0</sub> = 4π × 10<sup>-7</sup></i> T m/A)<br><br><br>A) 0.00251 T<br>B) 0.0101 T<br>C) 0.00201 T<br>D) 0.00503 T<br>E) 0.0201 T	<b>D) 0.00503 T</b><br><br><i>n = 800/0.40 = 2000</i> turns/m. <i>B = μ<sub>0</sub>nI = 4π × 10<sup>-7</sup> × 2000 × 2.0 = 0.00503</i> T.
