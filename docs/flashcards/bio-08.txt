bio08-023	<b>Weak interactions</b>: what is it?	<b>Weak interactions</b> are attractions between molecules, or between different parts of the same big molecule.	ImatPath Biology term
bio08-024	<b>hydrogen bond</b>: what is it?	A <b>hydrogen bond</b> is the attraction between a δ+ hydrogen (attached to O or N) and a δ− oxygen or nitrogen on <b>another</b> molecule, or elsewhere in the same one.	ImatPath Biology term
bio08-012	<small>Strong and weak bonds</small><br><b>Covalent bond</b><br><i>Based on · Strength?</i>	<b>Based on:</b> Shared electrons<br><b>Strength:</b> Strong	ImatPath Biology recall
bio08-013	<small>Strong and weak bonds</small><br><b>Ionic bond</b><br><i>Based on · Strength?</i>	<b>Based on:</b> Attraction between opposite charges<br><b>Strength:</b> Strong in dry crystals, <b>weak in water</b>	ImatPath Biology recall
bio08-014	<small>Strong and weak bonds</small><br><b>Hydrogen bond</b><br><i>Based on · Strength?</i>	<b>Based on:</b> Attraction between a slightly + H and a slightly − O or N<br><b>Strength:</b> Weak	ImatPath Biology recall
bio08-015	<small>Strong and weak bonds</small><br><b>Hydrophobic interaction</b><br><i>Based on · Strength?</i>	<b>Based on:</b> Non-polar groups pushed together by water<br><b>Strength:</b> Weak	ImatPath Biology recall
bio08-016	<small>Strong and weak bonds</small><br><b>Van der Waals forces</b><br><i>Based on · Strength?</i>	<b>Based on:</b> Short-lived attractions between any molecules that are very close<br><b>Strength:</b> Very weak	ImatPath Biology recall
bio08-017	<small>Hydrogen bonds and water</small><br><b>Cohesion</b><br><i>Why · Why it matters for life?</i>	<b>Why:</b> Water molecules stick to each other<br><b>Why it matters for life:</b> Water is pulled up the xylem of plants in unbroken columns. Surface tension lets insects walk on ponds	ImatPath Biology recall
bio08-018	<small>Hydrogen bonds and water</small><br><b>Adhesion</b><br><i>Why · Why it matters for life?</i>	<b>Why:</b> Water sticks to polar surfaces<br><b>Why it matters for life:</b> Helps water climb up narrow tubes in plants	ImatPath Biology recall
bio08-019	<small>Hydrogen bonds and water</small><br><b>High specific heat</b><br><i>Why · Why it matters for life?</i>	<b>Why:</b> Many hydrogen bonds must break before the temperature rises<br><b>Why it matters for life:</b> Oceans and our bodies stay at <b>stable temperatures</b>	ImatPath Biology recall
bio08-020	<small>Hydrogen bonds and water</small><br><b>High heat of vaporisation</b><br><i>Why · Why it matters for life?</i>	<b>Why:</b> Energy is needed to break the bonds<br><b>Why it matters for life:</b> <b>Sweating cools</b> the body	ImatPath Biology recall
bio08-021	<small>Hydrogen bonds and water</small><br><b>Ice floats</b><br><i>Why · Why it matters for life?</i>	<b>Why:</b> Frozen water forms an open lattice, less dense than liquid<br><b>Why it matters for life:</b> Lakes freeze from the top, so life survives below the ice	ImatPath Biology recall
bio08-022	<small>Hydrogen bonds and water</small><br><b>Good solvent</b><br><i>Why · Why it matters for life?</i>	<b>Why:</b> Its charges surround ions and polar molecules<br><b>Why it matters for life:</b> Reactions happen in water. Substances dissolve in blood and cytoplasm	ImatPath Biology recall
bio08-025	<small>Why weak bonds suit life</small><br><b>DNA</b>	the two strands are held together by hydrogen bonds between bases (<b>A–T: two bonds</b>, <b>G–C: three bonds</b>). The strands can be unzipped to be copied or read, then close up again.	ImatPath Biology key idea
bio08-026	<small>Why weak bonds suit life</small><br><b>Proteins</b>	hydrogen bonds make <b>alpha helices and beta sheets</b> (secondary structure). Ionic, hydrophobic and van der Waals interactions fold these into the final shape (tertiary structure). Heat or a change in pH breaks the weak bonds, and the protein <b>denatures</b>.	ImatPath Biology key idea
bio08-027	<small>Why weak bonds suit life</small><br><b>Enzymes</b>	the substrate fits the active site and is held by weak bonds. It can be released again after the reaction.	ImatPath Biology key idea
bio08-028	<small>Why weak bonds suit life</small><br><b>Cell signalling</b>	a hormone or drug binds its receptor by weak, reversible interactions.	ImatPath Biology key idea
bio08-029	<small>Answers & explanations</small><br><b>Covalent</b>	strong, sharing. <b>Hydrogen, ionic (in water), hydrophobic, van der Waals</b>: weak, reversible.	ImatPath Biology key idea
bio08-009	Key points: <b>Bond types</b>	• <b>Covalent</b>: strong, sharing. <b>Hydrogen, ionic (in water), hydrophobic, van der Waals</b>: weak, reversible.<br>• Many weak bonds together are strong.	ImatPath Biology summary
bio08-010	Key points: <b>Water</b>	• Polar. <b>Hydrogen bonds</b> between molecules give cohesion, adhesion, high specific heat, high heat of vaporisation, ice floating, good solvent.	ImatPath Biology summary
bio08-011	Key points: <b>In living things</b>	• DNA: <b>A–T 2 bonds, G–C 3 bonds</b>.<br>• Proteins: weak bonds hold folding. Heat or pH change → <b>denaturation</b>.<br>• Enzymes and receptors bind reversibly by weak interactions.	ImatPath Biology summary
bio08-001	Water rises up the xylem of a tall tree, and sweating cools the body. Which properties of water explain these two facts?<br><br><br>A) High density; high pH<br>B) Cohesion and adhesion; high heat of vaporisation<br>C) Low surface tension; low specific heat<br>D) High boiling point; very low viscosity<br>E) Polarity; being a non-solvent	<b>B) Cohesion and adhesion; high heat of vaporisation</b><br><br>Hydrogen bonds make water molecules stick to each other (cohesion) and to the xylem walls (adhesion), so a water column is pulled up. The same bonds give water a high heat of vaporisation, so evaporating sweat removes a lot of heat.	ImatPath Biology practice
bio08-003	A protein solution is boiled and loses its activity. Which interactions remain intact?<br><br><br>A) Peptide bonds<br>B) Hydrogen bonds<br>C) Ionic interactions<br>D) Hydrophobic interactions<br>E) Van der Waals forces	<b>A) Peptide bonds</b><br><br>Heat breaks the weak interactions that hold the secondary, tertiary and quaternary structure. The covalent peptide bonds of the primary structure are much stronger and remain, so the protein unfolds but is not broken into amino acids.	ImatPath Biology practice
bio08-005	Which order lists the interactions from the <b>weakest</b> to the <b>strongest</b>?<br><br><br>A) Covalent bond, hydrogen bond, van der Waals<br>B) Hydrogen bond, covalent bond, van der Waals<br>C) Van der Waals, covalent bond, hydrogen bond<br>D) Hydrogen bond, van der Waals, covalent bond<br>E) Van der Waals, hydrogen bond, covalent bond	<b>E) Van der Waals, hydrogen bond, covalent bond</b><br><br>Individual van der Waals forces are the weakest, hydrogen bonds are stronger, and covalent bonds are much stronger still. Many weak interactions together can still stabilise large molecules.	ImatPath Biology practice
bio08-007	Water is most dense at 4 °C, and ice is less dense than liquid water. What is the biological consequence for a pond in winter?<br><br><br>A) The pond freezes from the bottom upwards<br>B) All the water freezes at once<br>C) Fish are killed because the ice sinks<br>D) Ice forms at the surface and insulates the water below, so aquatic life survives<br>E) The pond evaporates faster	<b>D) Ice forms at the surface and insulates the water below, so aquatic life survives</b><br><br>In ice, hydrogen bonds hold molecules in an open lattice that is less dense than liquid water. The ice floats and insulates the water below, which stays liquid.	ImatPath Biology practice
