chem06-001	Formula to know: <b>The coordinate (dative) bond</b>	NH<sub>3</sub> + H<sup>+</sup> → NH<sub>4</sub><sup>+</sup>	ImatPath Chemistry formula
chem06-010	Key points: <b>Ionic and covalent</b>	• Ionic compounds are lattices. Formula = simplest neutral ratio of ions.<br>• Single, double and triple bonds: more pairs means <b>shorter and stronger</b>.<br>• Coordinate bond: both shared electrons from one atom (NH<sub>4</sub><sup>+</sup>).<br>• Melting a molecular solid breaks <b>forces between molecules</b>, not bonds inside.	ImatPath Chemistry summary
chem06-011	Key points: <b>Shape and polarity</b>	• Shape from electron pair repulsion: 180°, 120°, 109.5°, 107° (1 lone pair), 104.5° (2 lone pairs).<br>• <i>Δχ</i> below 0.4 non-polar, 0.4 to 1.8 polar, above 1.8 ionic.<br>• The more electronegative atom is <b>δ-</b>.<br>• A molecule is polar only if the dipoles do <b>not cancel</b>.	ImatPath Chemistry summary

chem06-008	Four solids have these properties. W: very high melting point, hard, does not conduct. X: high melting point, conducts only when molten. Y: conducts when solid, malleable. Z: low melting point, does not conduct. Which row matches W, X, Y, Z?<br><br><br>A) Ionic, giant covalent, metallic, simple molecular<br>B) Giant covalent, metallic, ionic, simple molecular<br>C) Simple molecular, ionic, metallic, giant covalent<br>D) Giant covalent, ionic, metallic, simple molecular<br>E) Metallic, ionic, giant covalent, simple molecular	<b>D) Giant covalent, ionic, metallic, simple molecular</b><br><br>W (very hard, high mp, no free charges) is giant covalent.
