chem19-001	<b>Isomers</b>: what is it?	<b>Isomers</b> are compounds with the <b>same molecular formula</b> but <b>different arrangements of atoms</b>. They are different substances, with different names and often different properties.	ImatPath Chemistry definition
chem19-002	<b>Enantiomers</b>: what is it?	<b>Enantiomers</b> are a pair of <b>non-superimposable mirror-image</b> isomers. A molecule with a chiral carbon is called <b>chiral</b>.	ImatPath Chemistry definition
chem19-014	<small>What isomers are</small><br><b>Structural isomers</b><br><i>Difference · Subtypes?</i>	<b>Difference:</b> The atoms are <b>joined in a different order</b><br><b>Subtypes:</b> Chain, position, functional group	ImatPath Chemistry recall
chem19-015	<small>What isomers are</small><br><b>Stereoisomers</b><br><i>Difference · Subtypes?</i>	<b>Difference:</b> The atoms are joined in the <b>same order</b> but are <b>arranged differently in space</b><br><b>Subtypes:</b> Geometric (cis-trans), optical	ImatPath Chemistry recall

chem19-022	<small>Geometric (cis-trans) isomers</small><br><b>cis (Z)</b>	the two similar groups are on the <b>same side</b>.	ImatPath Chemistry key idea
chem19-023	<small>Geometric (cis-trans) isomers</small><br><b>trans (E)</b>	they are on <b>opposite sides</b>.	ImatPath Chemistry key idea
chem19-024	<small>Geometric (cis-trans) isomers</small><br><b>But-2-ene</b>	yes. Each carbon has an H and a CH<sub>3</sub>.	ImatPath Chemistry key idea
chem19-025	<small>Geometric (cis-trans) isomers</small><br><b>But-1-ene</b>	no. One carbon has two H atoms, which are the same.	ImatPath Chemistry key idea
chem19-026	<small>Geometric (cis-trans) isomers</small><br><b>2-methylpropene</b>	no. One carbon has two CH<sub>3</sub> groups, which are the same.	ImatPath Chemistry key idea
chem19-027	<small>Answers & explanations</small><br><b>Chain</b>	straight or branched. More branched: lower boiling point. C<sub>4</sub>H<sub>10</sub> has 2, C<sub>5</sub>H<sub>12</sub> has 3, C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>14</sub> has 5.	ImatPath Chemistry key idea
chem19-028	<small>Answers & explanations</small><br><b>Position</b>	same group, different carbon (propan-1-ol, propan-2-ol).	ImatPath Chemistry key idea
chem19-029	<small>Answers & explanations</small><br><b>Functional group</b>	different group (ethanol and methoxymethane).	ImatPath Chemistry key idea
chem19-030	<small>Answers & explanations</small><br><b>Cis-trans</b>	C=C cannot rotate and each carbon has two different groups. Different physical properties.	ImatPath Chemistry key idea
chem19-031	<small>Answers & explanations</small><br><b>Optical</b>	a <b>chiral carbon</b> has four different groups. Enantiomers are non-superimposable mirror images. Same physical properties, <b>opposite rotation</b> of plane-polarised light. Racemic = 50:50, no rotation.	ImatPath Chemistry key idea
chem19-011	Key points: <b>Structural isomers (same formula, atoms joined differently)</b>	• <b>Chain</b>: straight or branched. More branched: lower boiling point. C<sub>4</sub>H<sub>10</sub> has 2, C<sub>5</sub>H<sub>12</sub> has 3, C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>14</sub> has 5.<br>• <b>Position</b>: same group, different carbon (propan-1-ol, propan-2-ol).<br>• <b>Functional group</b>: different group (ethanol and methoxymethane).	ImatPath Chemistry summary
chem19-012	Key points: <b>Stereoisomers (same bonds, different 3D arrangement)</b>	• <b>Cis-trans</b>: C=C cannot rotate and each carbon has two different groups. Different physical properties.<br>• <b>Optical</b>: a <b>chiral carbon</b> has four different groups. Enantiomers are non-superimposable mirror images. Same physical properties, <b>opposite rotation</b> of plane-polarised light. Racemic = 50:50, no rotation.	ImatPath Chemistry summary
chem19-013	Key points: <b>In the body</b>	• Enzymes and receptors are chiral. L-amino acids, D-sugars. Thalidomide. Natural unsaturated fats are cis.	ImatPath Chemistry summary

chem19-007	Which pair are <b>functional group isomers</b>?<br><br><br>A) Butane and 2-methylpropane<br>B) Propan-1-ol and propan-2-ol<br>C) cis-but-2-ene and trans-but-2-ene<br>D) Pentane and cyclopentane<br>E) Propanal and propanone	<b>E) Propanal and propanone</b><br><br>Propanal (aldehyde) and propanone (ketone) are both C<sub>3</sub>H<sub>6</sub>O but have different functional groups. Butane/2-methylpropane are chain isomers, the propanols are position isomers, the butenes are geometric isomers, and pentane/cyclopentane have different formulas.	ImatPath Chemistry practice
chem19-009	A pure enantiomer rotates plane-polarised light by +20°. A mixture contains 75% of this enantiomer and 25% of its mirror image. What is the observed rotation?<br><br><br>A) +20°<br>B) +15°<br>C) 0°<br>D) +10°<br>E) +5°	<b>D) +10°</b><br><br>Each enantiomer rotates by the same amount in opposite directions. The 25% of the (−) form cancels 25% of the (+) form, leaving an excess of <i>75 - 25 = 50%</i> of (+): <i>0.50 × 20° = +10°</i>. A 50 : 50 mixture (racemic) would give 0°.	ImatPath Chemistry practice
