bio24-021	<b>Recombinant DNA</b>: what is it?	<b>Recombinant DNA</b> is DNA made by <b>joining DNA from different sources</b>.	ImatPath Biology term
bio24-012	<small>Making recombinant DNA</small><br><b>Restriction enzymes (restriction endonucleases)</b><br><i>Job?</i>	Cut DNA at specific <b>recognition sequences</b> (often 4–8 bases, a <b>palindrome</b>). Many leave short single-stranded overhangs called <b>sticky ends</b>	ImatPath Biology recall
bio24-013	<small>Making recombinant DNA</small><br><b>DNA ligase</b><br><i>Job?</i>	Joins DNA fragments by forming phosphodiester bonds	ImatPath Biology recall
bio24-014	<small>Making recombinant DNA</small><br><b>Vector</b><br><i>Job?</i>	A DNA molecule that carries the gene into a host cell: usually a <b>plasmid</b>, or a virus	ImatPath Biology recall
bio24-015	<small>Making recombinant DNA</small><br><b>Reverse transcriptase</b><br><i>Job?</i>	Makes <b>cDNA</b> (complementary DNA) from mRNA. cDNA has <b>no introns</b>, so a bacterium can express it	ImatPath Biology recall
bio24-016	<small>Making recombinant DNA</small><br><b>Host cell</b><br><i>Job?</i>	Often <i>E. coli</i> or yeast	ImatPath Biology recall
bio24-017	<small>Applications and ethics</small><br><b>Medicine</b><br><i>Examples?</i>	Human <b>insulin</b>, growth hormone and clotting factors made by bacteria or yeast. <b>Vaccines</b> (hepatitis B vaccine from yeast, mRNA vaccines). Monoclonal antibodies. Gene therapy	ImatPath Biology recall
bio24-018	<small>Applications and ethics</small><br><b>Agriculture</b><br><i>Examples?</i>	<b>GM crops</b> resistant to insects (Bt maize) or herbicides, or enriched with vitamin A (<b>Golden Rice</b>)	ImatPath Biology recall
bio24-019	<small>Applications and ethics</small><br><b>Forensics</b><br><i>Examples?</i>	DNA profiling	ImatPath Biology recall
bio24-020	<small>Applications and ethics</small><br><b>Industry</b><br><i>Examples?</i>	Enzymes in detergents, biofuels	ImatPath Biology recall
bio24-022	<small>Making recombinant DNA</small><br><b>Transformation</b>	put the plasmid into bacteria (for example, by heat shock or electric shock).	ImatPath Biology key idea
bio24-023	<small>Answers & explanations</small><br><b>PCR</b>	95 °C denature, ~55 °C anneal, 72 °C extend (Taq). <b>2<sup>n</sup></b> copies.	ImatPath Biology key idea
bio24-024	<small>Answers & explanations</small><br><b>Electrophoresis</b>	DNA moves to <b>+</b>, small fragments farthest. <b>Sanger</b> sequencing (ddNTPs). <b>STR profiling</b> for forensics.	ImatPath Biology key idea
bio24-009	Key points: <b>Recombinant DNA</b>	• <b>Restriction enzyme</b> cuts (sticky ends, palindromes), <b>ligase</b> joins, <b>plasmid</b> vector, <b>cDNA</b> (no introns) from mRNA by reverse transcriptase.<br>• Transform bacteria; select with <b>antibiotic resistance</b> or <b>GFP</b> markers.	ImatPath Biology summary
bio24-010	Key points: <b>Techniques</b>	• <b>PCR</b>: 95 °C denature, ~55 °C anneal, 72 °C extend (Taq). <b>2<sup>n</sup></b> copies.<br>• <b>Electrophoresis</b>: DNA moves to <b>+</b>, small fragments farthest. <b>Sanger</b> sequencing (ddNTPs). <b>STR profiling</b> for forensics.	ImatPath Biology summary
bio24-011	Key points: <b>Editing and use</b>	• <b>CRISPR-Cas9</b> (guide RNA + Cas9 cut). <b>Gene therapy</b> (somatic, not inherited).<br>• Uses: insulin, vaccines, GM crops. Ethics: environment, privacy, embryo editing.	ImatPath Biology summary
bio24-001	A circular plasmid has 3 recognition sites for the restriction enzyme EcoRI. How many fragments result from complete digestion?<br><br><br>A) 2<br>B) 3<br>C) 4<br>D) 6<br>E) 1	<b>B) 3</b><br><br>In a circular molecule each cut opens the circle, but the number of fragments equals the number of cuts: 3 cuts give 3 fragments. (A linear DNA with 3 sites would give 4 fragments.)	ImatPath Biology practice
bio24-003	PCR starts from 5 molecules of target DNA. How many copies are there after 10 cycles, assuming 100% efficiency?<br><br><br>A) 5120<br>B) 50<br>C) 1024<br>D) 2560<br>E) 10 240	<b>A) 5120</b><br><br>Each cycle doubles the number of copies. After 10 cycles: <i>5 × 2<sup>10</sup> = 5 × 1024 = 5120</i>.	ImatPath Biology practice
bio24-005	A plasmid has an ampicillin resistance gene and a <i>lacZ</i> gene with a cloning site inside it. Cells are plated on ampicillin and X-gal (which turns blue when cleaved by β-galactosidase). Which colonies contain the recombinant plasmid?<br><br><br>A) Blue colonies that grow on ampicillin<br>B) Colonies that do not grow on ampicillin<br>C) Blue colonies only<br>D) Any colony that grows without ampicillin<br>E) White colonies that grow on ampicillin	<b>E) White colonies that grow on ampicillin</b><br><br>Growth on ampicillin shows the cell took up a plasmid. Inserting the gene into <i>lacZ</i> destroys β-galactosidase, so the colony stays white. Blue colonies have a plasmid without an insert.	ImatPath Biology practice
bio24-007	A child's DNA profile shows bands at positions 4 and 9. The mother has bands 4 and 7. Man 1 has bands 9 and 11 and man 2 has bands 5 and 8. Which man could be the father?<br><br><br>A) Man 2<br>B) Both men<br>C) Neither man<br>D) Man 1<br>E) It is impossible to say	<b>D) Man 1</b><br><br>Each band in the child comes from one parent. Band 4 comes from the mother, so band 9 must come from the father. Man 1 has band 9. Man 2 has neither 4 nor 9, so he is excluded.	ImatPath Biology practice
