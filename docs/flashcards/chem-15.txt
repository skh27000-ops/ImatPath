chem15-013	Key points: <b>Definitions</b>	• <b>Oxidation</b>: loss of electrons, oxidation number <b>up</b>. <b>Reduction</b>: gain of electrons, oxidation number <b>down</b>. (OIL RIG)<br>• The <b>oxidising agent is reduced</b>. The <b>reducing agent is oxidised</b>.<br>• A reaction is redox if any oxidation number changes.	ImatPath Chemistry summary
chem15-014	Key points: <b>Half-equations</b>	• Oxidation: Zn → Zn<sup>2+</sup> + 2e<sup>-</sup>. Reduction: Cu<sup>2+</sup> + 2e<sup>-</sup> → Cu.<br>• Balance in acid: atoms, then <b>O with H<sub>2</sub>O</b>, then <b>H with H<sup>+</sup></b>, then <b>charge with e<sup>-</sup></b>, then equalise electrons and add.<br>• Key one: MnO<sub>4</sub><sup>-</sup> + 8H<sup>+</sup> + 5e<sup>-</sup> → Mn<sup>2+</sup> + 4H<sub>2</sub>O.	ImatPath Chemistry summary
chem15-015	Key points: <b>Examples</b>	• Combustion, rusting, displacement and respiration are redox.<br>• Antioxidants (vitamins C and E) are reducing agents.<br>• Galvanising: zinc is oxidised in preference to iron.	ImatPath Chemistry summary

chem15-007	Chlorine reacts with cold sodium hydroxide: Cl<sub>2</sub> + 2NaOH → NaCl + NaClO + H<sub>2</sub>O. What kind of reaction is this?<br><br><br>A) Disproportionation: chlorine goes from 0 to −1 and to +1<br>B) Reduction only: chlorine goes from 0 to −1<br>C) Oxidation only: chlorine goes from 0 to +1<br>D) A neutralisation with no change in oxidation numbers<br>E) Disproportionation: oxygen goes from −2 to 0 and to −1	<b>A) Disproportionation: chlorine goes from 0 to −1 and to +1</b><br><br>In NaCl chlorine is −1 (reduced). In NaClO it is +1 (oxidised). The same element is both oxidised and reduced, which is <b>disproportionation</b>. Sodium (+1), oxygen (−2) and hydrogen (+1) do not change.	ImatPath Chemistry practice
chem15-009	In the reaction 2Fe<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub> + 3C → 4Fe + 3CO<sub>2</sub>, how many electrons are transferred according to the balanced equation?<br><br><br>A) 3<br>B) 4<br>C) 6<br>D) 24<br>E) 12	<b>E) 12</b><br><br>Carbon goes from 0 to +4: 3 C atoms lose <i>3 × 4 = 12</i> electrons. Iron goes from +3 to 0: 4 Fe atoms gain <i>4 × 3 = 12</i> electrons. The electrons lost and gained are equal.	ImatPath Chemistry practice
chem15-011	Ethanol (C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>5</sub>OH) is oxidised to ethanoic acid (CH<sub>3</sub>COOH) in the liver. How many electrons does one molecule lose?<br><br><br>A) 2<br>B) 3<br>C) 6<br>D) 4<br>E) 8	<b>D) 4</b><br><br>Average oxidation number of carbon: in C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>5</sub>OH <i>2x + 6 - 2 = 0</i> gives <i>x = -2</i>. In C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>4</sub>O<sub>2</sub>: <i>2x + 4 - 4 = 0</i> gives <i>x = 0</i>. Each of 2 carbons rises by 2, so 4 electrons are lost.	ImatPath Chemistry practice
