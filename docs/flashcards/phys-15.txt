phys15-001	<b>Capacitance</b>: what is it?	The <b>capacitance</b> <i>C</i> of a capacitor is the <b>charge stored per unit potential difference</b>: <i>C = (Q)/(V)</i>. The unit is the <b>farad (F)</b>: 1 F = 1 C/V.	ImatPath Physics definition
phys15-002	Formula to know: <b>The parallel-plate capacitor</b>	<i>C = (ε<sub>0</sub> A)/(d) &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; ε<sub>0</sub> = 8.85 × 10<sup>-12</sup> F/m</i>	ImatPath Physics formula
phys15-003	Formula to know: <b>Dielectrics</b>	<i>C = κ(ε<sub>0</sub>A)/(d)</i>	ImatPath Physics formula
phys15-004	Formula to know: <b>Work to charge a capacitor</b>	<i>U = (1)/(2)QV = (1)/(2)CV<sup>2</sup> = (Q<sup>2</sup>)/(2C)</i>	ImatPath Physics formula
phys15-005	Formula to know: <b>In parallel</b>	<i>C<sub>total</sub> = C<sub>1</sub> + C<sub>2</sub> + C<sub>3</sub> + …</i>	ImatPath Physics formula
phys15-006	Formula to know: <b>In series</b>	<i>(1)/(C<sub>total</sub>) = (1)/(C<sub>1</sub>) + (1)/(C<sub>2</sub>) + (1)/(C<sub>3</sub>) + …</i>	ImatPath Physics formula
phys15-007	Formula to know: <b>An RC circuit</b>	Charging: <i>Q = Q<sub>0</sub>(1 - e<sup>-t/RC</sup>) &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;</i> Discharging: <i>Q = Q<sub>0</sub> e<sup>-t/RC</sup></i><br><i>τ = RC</i> (the <b>time constant</b>, in seconds)	ImatPath Physics formula
phys15-016	Key points: <b>Capacitance</b>	• <i>C = Q/V</i> (farad). Parallel plates: <i>C = κε<sub>0</sub>A/d</i>. Dielectric increases <i>C</i>.<br>• Energy: <i>U = (1)/(2)QV = (1)/(2)CV<sup>2</sup> = Q<sup>2</sup>/2C</i>.	ImatPath Physics summary
phys15-017	Key points: <b>Combinations</b>	• Parallel: <i>C = C<sub>1</sub> + C<sub>2</sub> + …</i> (same <i>V</i>).<br>• Series: <i>1/C = 1/C<sub>1</sub> + 1/C<sub>2</sub> + …</i> (same <i>Q</i>), total smaller than the smallest.	ImatPath Physics summary
phys15-018	Key points: <b>RC circuits</b>	• <i>τ = RC</i>. Charging: <i>Q = Q<sub>0</sub>(1 - e<sup>-t/RC</sup>)</i>. Discharging: <i>Q = Q<sub>0</sub>e<sup>-t/RC</sup></i>.<br>• After one <i>τ</i>: 63% charged or 37% left. After 5<i>τ</i>: practically complete.	ImatPath Physics summary
phys15-008	A 6.0 <i>μ</i>F and a 3.0 <i>μ</i>F capacitor are connected in series, and this combination is connected in parallel with a 4.0 <i>μ</i>F capacitor. The whole network is connected across 12 V. What is the voltage across the 3.0 <i>μ</i>F capacitor?<br><br><br>A) 4.0 V<br>B) 8.0 V<br>C) 12 V<br>D) 6.0 V<br>E) 9.0 V	<b>B) 8.0 V</b><br><br>The series branch has <i>C = 6 × 3/9 = 2.0 μ</i>F and its voltage is 12 V, so its charge is <i>24 μ</i>C on <b>each</b> capacitor. <i>V<sub>3</sub> = Q/C = 24/3.0 = 8.0</i> V (and <i>V<sub>6</sub> = 4.0</i> V).	ImatPath Physics practice
phys15-010	A parallel-plate capacitor in air is charged to 200 V and then <b>disconnected</b> from the battery. A dielectric of constant <i>κ = 4</i> is slid between the plates, filling the gap. What is the new voltage?<br><br><br>A) 50 V<br>B) 800 V<br>C) 200 V<br>D) 100 V<br>E) 25 V	<b>A) 50 V</b><br><br>The charge is fixed. <i>C</i> becomes <i>4C</i>, so <i>V = Q/C</i> falls to <i>200/4 = 50</i> V. The stored energy <i>Q<sup>2</sup>/2C</i> also falls to a quarter.	ImatPath Physics practice
phys15-012	A 100 <i>μ</i>F capacitor is charged through a 50 k<i>Ω</i> resistor from a 9.0 V battery. What is the voltage across the capacitor after 5.0 s?<br><br><br>A) 3.31 V<br>B) 8.94 V<br>C) 9 V<br>D) 4.5 V<br>E) 5.69 V	<b>E) 5.69 V</b><br><br><i>τ = RC = 50 × 10<sup>3</sup> × 100 × 10<sup>-6</sup> = 5.0</i> s, so <i>t = τ</i>. Charging: <i>V = V<sub>0</sub>(1 - e<sup>-1</sup>) = 9.0 × 0.632 = 5.69</i> V.	ImatPath Physics practice
phys15-014	A 2.0 <i>μ</i>F capacitor is charged to 300 V and then disconnected from the source. The plates are slowly pulled apart until the separation has doubled. How much work is done?<br><br><br>A) 0.18 J<br>B) 0.045 J<br>C) 0.36 J<br>D) 0.090 J<br>E) 0	<b>D) 0.090 J</b><br><br>Charge stays constant and <i>C</i> halves, so <i>U = Q<sup>2</sup>/2C</i> doubles. <i>U<sub>1</sub> = (1)/(2)(2.0 × 10<sup>-6</sup>)(300)<sup>2</sup> = 0.090</i> J, <i>U<sub>2</sub> = 0.18</i> J. The work done <i>= U<sub>2</sub> - U<sub>1</sub> = 0.090</i> J.	ImatPath Physics practice
