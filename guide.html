By the ImatPath team · Updated November 2026 · 19 min read

The big picture

For 2026/27, 17 public universities in Italy teach Medicine and Surgery in English, and one exam, the IMAT, decides entry to all of them. It is a six-year degree (LM-41) that qualifies you to practise in Italy and is recognised in other EU countries under EU rules.

Public tuition depends on your family income, from about €156 to about €4,500 a year at most universities. Your main cost is usually rent.

Step by step

Choose your situation:

For students living outside the EU. Dates shown are those of 2026.

  1. Prepare your documents

    Have them translated into English or Italian:

    • passport-style photos;
    • a valid passport;
    • your transcript of records;
    • your school diploma.

    Legalisation, an apostille, a declaration of value or a CIMEA statement may be asked for: check your university’s call.

  2. Get your English certificate (B2)

    Whether you need B2 depends on the university. IELTS and TOEFL are the usual certificates. In some countries it is also needed for the visa: check with your embassy.

  3. Pre-evaluation, if your university asks

    Some universities check your diploma first: Messina, Bari, Marche, Turin, Milano-Bicocca, and Pavia if you sit the test there. It is not an admission. In 2026 the deadlines ran from 30 June to 11 September.

  4. Pre-enrol on Universitaly and get accepted

    Choose one university and submit your file. The university validates it (this can take up to 90 days) and sends it to the embassy. This acceptance of your file is not yet admission: your IMAT score decides that.

  5. Book your IMAT seat

    Register on Universitaly, choose your test centre and pay the fee (in 2026: 26 August to 9 September). Your first choice must be the same university as your pre-enrolment.

  6. Sit the IMAT and get ranked

    The exam is on paper, in September (in 2026: 29 September). Bring your ID and payment receipt. Scores came out on 8 October and the national ranking on 26 October.

  7. Enrol at your university

    If you are assigned a seat, enrol within 4 working days and confirm your interest at every update of the ranking.

  8. Apply for the study visa (type D)

    You need proof of money (at least €10,179.85 a year), accommodation, health insurance and your university validation. In 2026 the deadline was 30 November.

  9. Arrive in Italy

    Request your residence permit within 8 working days, finish your enrolment, and apply for the regional scholarship if you can.

For EU citizens, anyone legally resident in Italy, and EU-equivalent students (for example from Norway or Switzerland). Four steps, with no pre-enrolment and no visa. Dates shown are those of 2026.

  1. Register on Universitaly

    Rank the universities you want, choose a test centre and pay the fee (in 2026: 26 August to 9 September).

  2. Sit the IMAT

    In September. Bring your ID and payment receipt.

  3. Check the ranking

    Scores in October, then the national ranking. You need at least 20 points.

  4. Enrol

    If you are assigned a seat, enrol within 4 working days and confirm your interest at every update.

The 17 public universities

When you choose, look at four things:

  • The city. You will spend at least six years there.
  • The cost. A one-bedroom flat in the centre goes from about €475 to about €1,472 a month.
  • The score. Cut-offs differ by university, for EU and non-EU, and change every year.
  • Where you actually study. Milano-Bicocca teaches in Bergamo, Parma in Piacenza, Turin in Orbassano, and one Padova programme is in Venice.

The North is usually pricier, with strong research. The South and the islands are more affordable, with generally lower cut-offs.

The IMAT exam

The IMAT has 60 multiple-choice questions to answer in 100 minutes, for a maximum of 90 points.

SectionQuestions
Biology23
Chemistry15
Physics and mathematics13
Logical reasoning5
Reading and general knowledge4

How the score works

You get +1.5 for a correct answer, 0 for a blank one and −0.4 for a wrong one. For example, 40 correct, 10 wrong and 10 blank gives 40 × 1.5 − 10 × 0.4 = 56 out of 90.

Every score goes into one national ranking. Your rank and your university preferences decide your seat.

How to study

Start with Biology and Chemistry: together they are 38 of the 60 questions. Study the syllabus chapter by chapter, practise each chapter right after you learn it, and keep the last months for full timed mock exams. You can download past papers for free to see the real level.

Documents

For pre-enrolment on Universitaly, prepare:

  • a valid passport (check the expiry date);
  • a recent passport-style photo;
  • diplomas and transcripts, translated into Italian or English;
  • a language certificate, only if your university asks for it.

Your name must match your passport everywhere. Ask your university whether it accepts a declaration of value or a CIMEA statement. If your IB or A-level results are not final yet, upload provisional results: the final diploma is needed before enrolment.

What goes wrong

  • Documents that do not meet that university’s criteria.
  • Wrong personal data: you cannot edit it, you have to send a support ticket on Universitaly.
  • Pre-enrolling at two universities. Non-EU applicants may choose only one.
  • A missed university deadline.

Three mistakes that can get you rejected
  1. Your pre-enrolment university is not your IMAT first choice.
  2. You miss your university’s deadline.
  3. Your documents are incomplete, or the name does not match your passport.

Regional scholarships

A regional right-to-study agency, not the university, can give a tuition waiver, subsidised or free housing, daily meals and a yearly cash grant, based on family income (ISEE). In Lazio, for example, the grant reaches €7,557 a year for students living away from home with an ISEE below €28,339. Every region has its own rules and dates.

Private universities

Private universities charge flat fees of about €16,000 to €23,000 a year and run their own admission tests (HUMAT, San Raffaele, UniCamillus, Cattolica, Campus Bio-Medico). They are not part of the IMAT.

The student visa

Non-EU students apply for a type D study visa after their pre-enrolment is validated. For 2026/27 the visa file asked for:

  • Money: at least €10,179.85 a year, from legal and traceable sources. A scholarship counts only with an award letter.
  • Housing: confirmed accommodation. Only applying for a residence hall does not count.
  • Health insurance valid in Italy.
  • Your file: university validation, passport, and a provisional diploma certificate if you have no diploma yet.

A validated pre-enrolment is not an admission, and the embassy decides on the visa.

Questions students asked

Which documents and deadlines matter most?

Keep two things: your checklist (passport, photo, diplomas and transcripts, translated) and your own university’s deadline. Put both in your calendar.

Is the IB diploma accepted?

Yes, with a declaration of value or a CIMEA statement. Each university sets its details, so read its call.

Is there a minimum school grade?

There is no national minimum: non-EU applicants are ranked on the IMAT score. You need a diploma after 12 years of schooling. Some universities may set minimum grades.

How do I choose a university?

Pick a city you can live in, check the rent, then look at last year’s cut-offs.

Your next steps

  1. Try one past paper to measure your level.
  2. Shortlist two or three universities.
  3. Check your passport and start your diploma translation and legalisation.
  4. Study the syllabus chapter by chapter.
  5. Watch your university’s page, Universitaly and the ministry (MUR) for the 2027 dates.
